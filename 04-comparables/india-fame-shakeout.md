# India — what happened when the subsidy ended, and who won

**Section D of the research plan. Recorded 26-Aug-2026.**

India is the closest available analogue to Pakistan: same subcontinent, same 100–125cc commuter
baseline, same price sensitivity, and a subsidy scheme structurally similar to Pakistan's NEV
cost-sharing. It ran the entire cycle — boom, subsidy withdrawal, fraud investigation, shakeout
— between 2022 and 2026. **Pakistan is at the start of that curve.**

**Evidence grade: press, multiple independent outlets, mutually consistent.** Ola's figures are
published quarterly results. Not yet read from filings — that upgrade is available.

---

## 🔴 The headline finding, which inverts the obvious lesson

**The market survived the subsidy collapse. The subsidy-era winners did not.**

Indian e-2W registrations rose **53% in H1 2026** and **75.5% year-on-year in June 2026**, and
electric crossed **10% of the two-wheeler market** — *after* per-vehicle support had been cut to
a fraction of its former level.

But the leaderboard was replaced entirely. **The companies that dominated under subsidy are
gone; the companies that won are the legacy petrol motorcycle manufacturers.**

| June 2026 | Units | Share | What they are |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **TVS Motor** | 46,999 | **25%** | Legacy ICE motorcycle giant |
| **Bajaj Auto** | 43,234 | **23%** | Legacy ICE motorcycle giant |
| **Ather Energy** | 31,143 | 16% | EV pure-play — the one survivor |
| **Hero MotoCorp** | 21,761 | 11% | Legacy ICE motorcycle giant |
| **Ola Electric** | 16,144 | 8% | EV pure-play — collapsing |

**TVS, Bajaj, Hero and Ather took 95.6% of all incremental registrations in H1 2026.**

**This is the finding that matters most for Pakistan.** Demand is real and survives subsidy
withdrawal. But it is captured by whoever already has dealers, service and brand trust — which
in Pakistan means **Atlas Honda, United, Road Prince**, not a new entrant.

## The three-act story

### Act 1 — FAME-II built a market on price support
Generous per-vehicle subsidies drew in a wave of new entrants. **Hero Electric and Okinawa held
31% combined share in 2022.**

### Act 2 — the compliance blow-up
The Serious Fraud Investigation Office acted against **Hero Electric, Okinawa Autotech and
Benling India** over roughly **INR 2.97bn** in subsidy claims, finding they had *"deceptively
shown compliance"* with localisation guidelines. The Ministry of Heavy Industries demanded
repayment of about **INR 469 crore**.

Consequences: **Hero Electric and Benling laid off over 80% of permanent staff** and lost most
of their dealers. Beneficiary two-wheelers under FAME-II were cut from ~989,000 to under
564,000. A further ~INR 1,200 crore of claims went unpaid, with **model deregistration and
debarment**.

🔴 **The transferable risk: claiming a localisation-linked subsidy creates clawback exposure.**
Pakistan's 1% duty concession is gated on EDB certification and annual input reconciliation
(`03-pakistan/edb-certification.md`) — the same structural shape. **Getting the paperwork wrong
is not a fine; in India it was a company-ending event.**

### Act 3 — the subsidy shrank and the market grew anyway
FAME-II's expiry cut e-2W sales **53% in a single month**. Its successor, **PM E-DRIVE**, pays
far less — **₹2,500 per kWh capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle** from 1 April 2025 — and has been
extended in short increments (latest to 31 July 2026) with **no confirmed successor**. The
government is deliberately weaning the market off support.

**And volumes still grew 53–75%.** Fuel costs and product maturity carried it once price support
stopped.

## 🔴 Why Ola Electric lost, and why it is the most important paragraph here

Ola was the best-funded, most-hyped pure-play. It had capital, product, brand and **over 50%
market share in April 2024**. By H1 2026 it was at **6.8%**, with registrations down 44.1% and
retail sales down more than 51%. Q4 FY26 revenue was **₹265 crore, −57% YoY**, on a **₹500 crore
quarterly loss**; the full-year loss was **₹1,833 crore**.

**It did not lose on price, product or capital. It lost on service.**

- **10,644 complaints** to India's National Consumer Helpline between September 2023 and August
  2024 — manufacturing defects, long service waits, and **warranty claims refused**.
- Scooters photographed **queued at service centres, some gathering dust** awaiting parts.
- 🔴 **The Goa state government suspended Ola's vehicle registrations** after a Central Consumer
  Protection Authority probe into inadequate after-sales service and **unavailable spare parts**.
- It has since reversed its direct-to-consumer strategy and turned to dealers, opening 3,200
  stores in a single day — a catch-up move, not a plan.

Meanwhile TVS, Bajaj and Ather gained *"aided in part by wider dealer and service networks that
could absorb demand faster than Ola's own stores."*

### What this does to the kill criteria

- **K8 (parts and service) is not a secondary criterion. It is the one that decides.** The
  Indian market's largest player was destroyed by parts availability and service capacity while
  holding 50% share. K8's current threshold — PKR 7.5m for parts stock and training — was
  reasoned as a capital ceiling. **India suggests underspending here is the failure mode, not
  the saving.**
- **K5 gains a new consequence.** Refused warranty claims did not just cost money; they drew a
  regulator, and **a state deregistered the vehicles.** Warranty failure can remove your right
  to sell. That is not in the criteria and should be.
- **K11 (defensible position) hardens further.** The defensible position in India turned out to
  be *an existing dealer and service network*. In Pakistan those are owned by Atlas Honda,
  United and Road Prince — and **Road Prince already carries Yadea.**

## Transferable lessons for Pakistan

1. **Demand survives subsidy withdrawal; entrants often do not.** Model the business with zero
   concession — India proves the market persists, and also proves who gets to serve it.
2. **The winners will be incumbent petrol manufacturers.** Pakistan's equivalents have the
   dealer estates, the service networks and the balance sheets. Expect Atlas Honda to do what
   TVS and Bajaj did. **Plan against that, not around it.**
3. **Subsidy compliance carries clawback risk.** EDB certification and annual reconciliation
   must be treated as a legal obligation, not administration.
4. **Service capacity is the binding constraint on growth**, not supply. Selling faster than you
   can service is how a 50%-share leader became an 8% one.
5. **One pure-play survived — Ather** — on engineering credibility and a controlled network
   rather than on volume. If a new entrant wins, that is the shape it takes: **narrow, deep,
   service-first**, not cheap and wide.

## What would change this reading

- Reading Ola's, TVS's and Ather's actual filings rather than press summaries.
- Pakistani dealer-network counts for Atlas Honda and United — the scale of what a new entrant
  faces is currently unquantified.
- Whether Pakistan's NEV subsidy carries an equivalent localisation-compliance clawback in its
  legal text, as opposed to structurally resembling one.

## Open items

1. Quantify Pakistani incumbent dealer and service networks. **This is now a priority item** —
   it is the number that decides K11.
2. Add a kill criterion, or extend K5, for **regulatory action arising from after-sales
   failure**.
3. Revisit K8's PKR 7.5m ceiling in light of the Ola outcome.
