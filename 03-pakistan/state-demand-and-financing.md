# The state is manufacturing demand at 5× national production

**Recorded 28-Aug-2026.** This is the largest demand finding in the project, and it partly
supersedes earlier subsidy figures.

## 🔴 The numbers that matter

| | |
|---|---:|

| **Pakistan's total e-2W production, FY2024-25** | **22,404 units** |

🔴 **Read those two rows together. The government is subsidising 116,000 machines in a year in

**Source:** PM's Electric Bike and Rickshaw Scheme 2026, under the **PAVE** programme
(*Pakistan Accelerated Vehicle Electrification*), launched 23-Feb-2026. Production figures are
**EDB's own data**. Grade: press reporting of official programmes — <span>consistent across
outlets, but not read from an EDB publication.</span>

**Eligibility is broader than the student schemes suggest:** Pakistani citizen, valid CNIC, **age
18–65**, valid driving licence or learner's permit. **Nationwide — Sindh included.**

## 🔴 CORRECTION — the subsidy is PKR 80,000, not 65,000

Every earlier file in this workspace used **PKR 65,000** from the NEV Policy 2025-30. The operative
figure under PAVE is **PKR 80,000**, and the subsidised machines are reported to land
**PKR 50,000–60,000 below comparable market models.**

⚠️ **`05-numbers/tco-model.md` and the artifact's subsidised-payback case both need re-running.**
A larger subsidy shortens the subsidised payback below the 3.9 months already modelled.

## 🔴 THE EDB TENDER — a 500-unit batch structure, and it was reachable

**PPRA tender TS0000007069E — "Fast Track Adoption – 100,000 E-Bikes (2-Wheelers) (Under NEV
Policy 2025–30)"**, issued by the **Engineering Development Board**, Ministry of Industries &
Production.

| | |
|---|---|
| Scope | **100,000 e-bikes** |
| Structure | 🔴 **200 batches of 500 units each** |
| Timeline | **90 days, fast-track deployment** |
| Invited | **EOIs from all EV 2-wheeler OEMs / Assemblers** |
| Basis | *"available production capacity and **CKD inventory**"* |
| Condition | *"local value addition in line with NEV Policy 2025–30"* |
| **Deadline** | ⚠️ **June 2026 — ALREADY CLOSED** |

🟢 **The batch size is the finding.** 500 units is **2.5× the 200-machine fleet this project
modelled** and **10× the 50-unit supplier MOQ** — an order of exactly the scale a small entrant
could serve, and the tender explicitly leans on **existing CKD inventory**.

🔴 **But the window shut in June 2026.** Whether further batches or a second EOI round follow is
**the single most valuable thing to ask EDB** — and it is a phone call, not a research task.
It also reframes `01-question/edb-call-script.md`: the call is no longer only about certification
requirements, it is about **whether there is a procurement pipeline to sell into.**

## 🔴 The fleet thesis is no longer novel — foodpanda is already doing it

**foodpanda Pakistan has launched an EV delivery fleet**, in partnership with the **Government of
Punjab** and **Wasl Investment Finance**, an **SECP-regulated NBFC**. Launched in **Lahore**.
Riders access **EV financing through Wasl**.

**Wasl's own positioning:** operating since 2020 under an Investment Finance Services licence,
running **Wasl Mobility Modaraba**; states it provides *"cash flow based financing … structured in
ways to avoid the need for any form of hard collateral"* for **micro consumers and small
businesses**. It previously partnered with **YES Electromotive** on EV accessibility (2023).

### What this does to the thesis — stated honestly

🟢 **It validates the structure.** `fleet-model.md` argued the answer to rider affordability is a
platform-plus-financier arrangement collecting against rider earnings rather than lending to
individuals. **A delivery platform and an NBFC have independently built exactly that.** The model
is not speculative.

🔴 **And it removes first-mover advantage in Punjab.** The platform relationship — the scarcest
asset in the whole plan — is already taken there, by the largest player, with government backing.

⚠️ **Three things are NOT yet established, and they decide how much this matters:**
1. **Whether it is financing-to-own or rental.** Reporting says *"access EV financing"*, which reads
   as lease/hire-purchase to ownership, **not** a fleet the operator owns and rents. **Those are
   different businesses.** `[VERIFY]`
2. **Whether it extends to Karachi.** Everything reported is **Lahore and Punjab**, alongside a
   Punjab-government partnership that does not exist in Sindh.
3. **Scale.** No unit count, no target, no terms were disclosed in any report found.

🔴 **The obvious opening is the one Punjab's scheme cannot reach: Karachi, and Bykea.** Sindh has
no provincial e-bike scheme, foodpanda's programme is Punjab-anchored, and Bykea — 30,000+ riders —
has no announced EV partner. **That is a question for Bykea, not for more desk research.**

## The industry this would be entering

**EDB has issued manufacturing licences to 34 companies for electric two- and three-wheelers**
(50+ across all EV categories).

🔴 **Do the arithmetic: 34 licensed companies produced 22,404 units between them — an average of
about 660 units each per year.** Even allowing for a skewed distribution, this is a **fragmented,
sub-scale industry**, not a consolidated one.

**Two readings, and both are true:**
- 🟢 **EDB certification is demonstrably passable.** 34 companies hold a licence. The gate this
  project has treated as the central unknown has been cleared 34 times.
- 🔴 **A licence is not a factory, and it is not volume.** 660 units a year is a workshop. The
  state is about to push 116,000 subsidised units through an industry whose entire output is
  22,404 — so **either the subsidy pulls in imports, or the incumbents scale hard, or the
  programme underdelivers.** `[PLACEHOLDER: how the 116,000 are actually to be supplied]` — the
  scheme announcement does not say.
