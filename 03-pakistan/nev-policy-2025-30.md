# NEV Policy 2025-30 — read from the primary source

**Recorded 25-Aug-2026.** Source: *New Energy Vehicles Policy 2025-30*, Ministry of Industries
& Production, obtained from the Engineering Development Board site and saved at
`06-sources/NEV-Policy-2025-30-Pakistan-MoIP.pdf` with extracted text alongside it.

This supersedes press reporting on every point where the two disagree. Quotes below are
verbatim from the policy text.

---

## 🔴 The finding that changes the case

> "local assembly and production capacity of electric two and three-wheeler has risen to two 2
> million vehicles per annum. **However, much of this capacity is heavily underutilized.**"

**Pakistan already has ~2 million units a year of electric two- and three-wheeler assembly
capacity, and most of it sits idle.** The government's own policy says the demand-side measures
exist to fill capacity that already exists.

This is decisive for **K11 (defensible position)** and it points the opposite way to the
obvious plan. Building another assembly line adds supply to a market whose problem is
underutilisation. **The bottleneck in Pakistan is not the ability to assemble — it is demand,
distribution, brand and after-sales.** Any entry thesis resting on "assemble locally because
policy favours it" has to answer why idle capacity has not already absorbed the opportunity.

---

## 🔴 The concessions had an expiry date — but read the correction first

> ⚠️ **SUPERSEDED IN PART, 25-Aug-2026.** The quotes below are accurate, but the conclusion
> drawn from them — "K7 has already fired" — was wrong. The **Finance Act 2026 extended the EV
> CKD concessions by one year, to 30 June 2027.** The regime did not lapse. See
> [`tariff-structure-current.md`](tariff-structure-current.md) for what actually governs.
>
> **The lesson is worth more than the error:** a policy document's stated end date is not the
> law in force. The annual finance act overtakes it, and must be checked before concluding
> anything expired.

> "existing incentives for NEVs as provided under the AIDEP 2021–26 shall remain in effect till
> the end of the policy period i.e **June 30, 2026**. Thereafter, all applicable tariffs
> including customs duty, additional customs duty, and regulatory duty shall be gradually
> adjusted in accordance with the principles laid out in the NTP 2025–30."

> "special duty regimes currently applicable to the auto sector, including those under the **5th
> Schedule to the Customs Act, 1969 will be gradually phased out by 2030**."

> "any preferential sales tax treatment extended to localized components shall also be
> **gradually withdrawn by 2030**."

> "the lower rate of sales tax applied on sale of certain imported parts will be adjusted to
> bring it at par with the rate applicable to similar locally manufactured parts."

**Today is 25-Aug-2026. That date is nearly two months past.** Every favourable duty and tax
figure circulating in trade press describes the AIDEP 2021-26 regime, which was scheduled to
end on 30 June 2026. Treat all of them as **expired until re-confirmed against the current
tariff schedule.**

MoIP undertook to publish a transition roadmap "including post June 2026 tariff structure for
NEVs". **Finding that document is now the highest-priority open item** — no landed-cost model
is possible without it, and building one on pre-July-2026 rates would produce a confident
wrong answer.

## The subsidy is real, smaller than reported, and designed to shrink

> "the initial reference amount for cost sharing shall be **PKR 65,000** and PKR. 400,000 for
> two and three wheelers respectively."

> "the subsidy per vehicle will be gradually reduced from the second year of the policy onward.
> It will be reduced to **80% in the third year of the policy, 60% in the fourth year and 40% in
> the fifth year**."

⚠️ **Press reported PKR 80,000 per electric motorcycle; the policy says PKR 65,000.** The two
may describe different schemes — the PKR 80,000 figure was attributed to a separate PAVE
programme — but until that is resolved, **PKR 65,000 is the sourced number and the press figure
is not.**

Budgeted cost-sharing for two-wheelers: **PKR 85.05 billion** across the five years.

## Adoption targets (Table 2 of the policy)

| Year | 2-wheeler target |
|---|---:|
| 2025-26 | 116,053 |
| 2026-27 | 246,728 |
| 2027-28 | 393,408 |
| 2028-29 | 557,590 |
| 2029-30 | 740,898 |
| **Total** | **2,054,677** |

Headline ambition: NEVs at 30% of new vehicle sales by 2030; 50% by 2040; 100% by 2050.

🔴 **Discount these targets.** The policy opens by admitting its predecessor missed:

> "The 2019 policy failed to meet its five years targets."

and that its supply-side measures "have not been fully implemented till now." A target from a
ministry that missed the last set is an intention, not a forecast, and must never be used as a
demand assumption in a cost model.

## Charging

40 Level-3 fast-charging sites in phase one along motorways and the N5, within six months of
approval; 3,000 stations by 2030, including **swapping stations for two- and three-wheelers**.
Commercial charging tariff fixed at **PKR 39.7/kWh**.

## What this does to the kill criteria

- **K7 (policy dependence) is no longer hypothetical — it has already fired once.** The regime
  every public figure describes expired on 30 June 2026, and the replacement is unpublished or
  unfound. The policy also commits to phasing out the special duty regimes entirely by 2030.
  **Any model must be run with no concessions at all**, because that is the direction of travel
  the government has written down.
- **K11 (defensible position) got much harder.** Two million units of idle assembly capacity
  means capacity is not scarce. Whatever the moat is, it is not a factory.
- **K6 (landed cost) is blocked** until the post-June-2026 tariff structure is found.

## Open items this creates

1. Find the MoIP post-June-2026 NEV tariff structure and the AIDEP successor policy.
2. Resolve PKR 65,000 vs PKR 80,000 — different schemes, or a press error?
3. Establish who holds the 2m units of installed capacity and how idle it actually is.
