# EDB certification — what it actually requires

**Recorded 26-Aug-2026.** This is the gate sitting upstream of the 1% duty rate. Without EDB
certification there is no concession, so this is a precondition of the whole assembly route.

Primary sources, both saved in `06-sources/`:
- **SRO 656(I)/2006**, updated to 30.06.2021 — `SRO-656-I-2006-updated-30-06-2021-Auto-OEMs.pdf`
- **AIDEP 2021-26**, Ministry of Industries & Production — `AIDEP-2021-26-Pakistan-MoIP.pdf`

---

## 🔴 The finding that matters most

**SRO 656 contains no electric-vehicle provisions at all.** Its facility schedules are
A. Cars & LCVs · B. Heavy Commercial Vehicles · C. Tractors · D. Motorcycle / Motorcycle
Rickshaw · E. Stroke Auto Rickshaw. The only occurrence of the word "electric" in the entire
document is *"Electric Hoist"*, describing a crane.

**And Section D — the motorcycle schedule an electric two-wheeler assembler would be measured
against — is written entirely for internal combustion.** It mandates, verbatim:

> **"ENGINE ASSEMBLY & TESTING"** — an assembly line with fixtures for *"Crank Cases and Crank
> Case Covers"*, *"Complete Cylinder with Crankshaft and Connecting Rod"*, *"Spindle Kick
> Starter"*, *"Spark Plug, Rocker Arm and Tappet adjusting"*, *"Cover Cylinder Head and Oil
> Filling"*, plus *"Engine number Punching Arrangement"* and engine leakage testing.

Final inspection additionally requires an **"Emission Tester"**.

**An electric motorcycle has no crankcase, no crankshaft, no connecting rod, no spark plug, no
tappets, no oil filling and no emissions.** The written criteria cannot be satisfied by the
product, because the schedule predates it.

### Why this is a live risk, not a technicality

The 1% duty depends on EDB certifying facilities against a schedule that does not describe an
electric vehicle. That means **certification rests on EDB's discretion in applying an obsolete
instrument** — not on meeting a published, checkable standard.

Practical consequences:

- **You cannot self-assess eligibility before applying.** There is no bar to measure yourself
  against, so cost and timeline are unknowable in advance.
- **Discretion is not portable.** What one applicant was accepted on does not bind the next.
- **It may explain the licence-versus-factory gap** — 50+ licences issued but reportedly only
  about six actually manufacturing. Worth testing against that hypothesis.

⚠️ This is exactly the kind of thing that surfaces *after* capital is committed. **Ask EDB
directly how Section D is applied to electric two-wheelers before anything else in this
project proceeds.** It is a single question and it governs everything downstream.

## The requirements as written

### 1. Prerequisite registration
Must be an *"importer-cum-assembler or manufacturer registered under the **Sales Tax Act,
1990**"*.

### 2. Minimum in-house facilities — SRO 656, Section D
Verified **on site** by EDB. Seven areas:

| # | Facility | Required equipment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frame welding shop** | Welding jigs · spot welder · MIG welder · frame/chassis inspection jigs · boring machine for the head pipe |
| 2 | **Body paint shop** | Cleaning, degreasing, de-rusting, phosphate prep · spray booths with preparation and flash-off · finish coat · **baking ovens** |
| 3 | **Engine assembly & testing** | 🔴 Entirely ICE-specific — see above |
| 4 | **Component testing** | Surface plate · dimensional instruments · material hardness tester · destructive test rig · coating thickness meter · run-out accuracy testing |
| 5 | **Final assembly** | Full-height frame assembly fixture · pneumatic guns · torque wrenches · compressor · press/pneumatic jig · frame number punching · material handling |
| 6 | **Final inspection** | Brake test bench · speedometer test bench · headlight aiming · vehicle alignment · **emission tester** · noise tester |
| 7 | **Storage** | Separate: vendorised/in-house parts · CKD parts · finished goods |

Even setting the engine shop aside, items 1, 2, 5 and 6 are **a real factory** — welding,
paint with baking ovens, and a test line. This is not a warehouse operation.

### 3. Component list approval
Every imported part must be listed **by part number and description**, verified by **EDB or
IOCO** (Input Output Co-efficient Organization), and **uploaded to WeBOC**, the customs system.
Nothing enters at the concessionary rate unless it is on that approved list.

### 4. Annual reconciliation — a hard deadline
> *"records along with reconciliation account of all inputs used in assembly or manufacture of
> vehicles shall be submitted to the EDB or IOCO on an annual basis latest by **15th August**
> each year, **failing which the manufacturing certificate of the firm shall not be
> revalidated**."*

**Miss 15 August and the certificate lapses.** EDB or IOCO may also suspend or decline to
revalidate for non-submission "or for any other reason to be recorded in writing".

### 5. Ongoing monitoring
EDB monitors imports and production on a continuing basis and can cancel or suspend the
production certificate and importable quota lists on breach.

## The on-ramp AIDEP created for two- and three-wheelers

AIDEP 2021-26 provided a pilot route that a first-time entrant could genuinely use:

> *"**10 CBU units (for each variant) to be allowed at 50% of prevailing custom duty** to the
> extent of maximum 200 units to 2-3 wheeler segment including scooties, motorcycles, rickshaws
> and 3 wheel loaders."*
>
> *"The maximum units to be imported collectively... **not to exceed 200 units per company**
> even in case it has more than 20 different variants. **Subsequent manufacturing within 2 years
> of import will be compulsory.**"*
>
> *"Import to be **recommended by EDB**... **for approval by MoIP.**"*

**Import up to 200 units at half duty to test the market, then manufacture within two years.**
That is a legitimate, documented "prove it before you build it" path — and it fits a
capital-constrained first entry far better than either full CBU importing or building a plant
outright.

🔴 **But AIDEP 2021-26 expired on 30 June 2026.** Whether this pilot survives into the current
regime is unconfirmed. The known continuation is the **four-wheeler** version (100 CBU units at
50% duty). **Confirm the two-wheeler pilot still exists before building any plan on it** — it
is the single most useful provision found so far, and it may no longer be there.
