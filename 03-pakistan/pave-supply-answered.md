## 🔴 CORRECTION — the subsidy is TWO-TIER, and I reported only the higher number

Every file in this workspace, and the published artifact, states the PAVE subsidy as
**PKR 80,000 per motorcycle.** The official portal shows **two tiers**:

| Vehicle | **Financed** | **Self-finance** |
|---|---:|---:|
| **E-bike** | **PKR 50,000** | **PKR 80,000** |
| E-rickshaw / loader | PKR 200,000 | PKR 400,000 |

🔴 **PKR 80,000 is the SELF-FINANCE subsidy. A buyer taking the financed route gets PKR 50,000** —
**37.5% less than this research has been reporting.**

⚠️ **This matters for the demand case.** The subsidised-payback scenario should be run at
**PKR 50,000** for anyone using instalments — which is most of the target market, since the whole
premise is that riders cannot pay upfront. **The 80,000 case applies to cash buyers.**

**Also corrected:** the earlier NEV Policy figure of PKR 65,000 sits *between* the two tiers, so it
was neither right nor simply superseded.

## 🟢 THE SUPPLY QUESTION IS ANSWERED

After four rounds of searching, the portal states it plainly:

> **"Only models approved by the Engineering Development Board (EDB) will be offered"** — vehicles
> are *"shortlisted based on safety, performance, and compliance standards."*

🔴 **So PAVE is not an import channel. It is a demand channel routed through EDB-licensed
manufacturers** — the same 34 companies that produced 22,404 units between them last year.

**Read the consequence carefully, because it cuts both ways:**

🟢 **For an EDB-certified assembler, PAVE is a state-funded order book.** 116,000 subsidised units
against a national production of 22,404 means the licensees are the bottleneck, not the demand.
**This is the strongest argument yet for pursuing EDB certification rather than importing CBUs.**

🔴 **For anyone without EDB certification, PAVE is a wall, not a door.** The subsidy makes approved
models cheaper than anything an uncertified importer can land. **It converts EDB certification from
a duty-rate question into a market-access question.**

⚠️ **Still not established:** whether the 34 licensees can physically produce 116,000 units, or
whether approved models will be assembled from imported CKD at volume — which is where a CKD kit
supplier would fit. `[PLACEHOLDER]`

# Lithium import scale, and three models added to the catalogue — 29-Aug-2026

## 🔴 Pakistan already imports lithium at GWh scale, on the exact HS code Route B uses

| | |
|---|---:|
| Monthly imports, **HS 8507.6000**, Jan 2024 | **42 MWh** |
| Monthly imports, **April 2026** | **652 MWh** — a record |
| Growth | **+1,640%** |
| Battery-storage imports, 2025 | **4.6 GWh**, +220% YoY |
| Cumulative to end-2025 | **7.6 GWh** |
| Current annualised rate | **~5.86 GWh** |
| Projected 2030 | **8.7 GWh** |

⚠️ **Most of this is SOLAR STORAGE, not EV.** These are battery-energy-storage-system imports driven
by Pakistan's solar boom and, reportedly, by Middle East disruption accelerating energy
independence. **Do not read 652 MWh as EV battery demand.**

🟢 **Two things it does establish:**

1. **The import channel already exists at scale on the same tariff line.** Supplier relationships,
   freight routes, DG handling and customs precedent for lithium at GWh volume are in place. **The
   valuation ruling exists precisely because that volume is large** — this is not an untrodden path.
2. **It sizes the EV programme honestly.** **116,000 e-bikes at ~2.5 kWh is roughly 290 MWh — about
   5% of Pakistan's annualised lithium imports.** 🔴 **The state's flagship EV scheme is a rounding
   error beside the solar-storage market.** Anyone modelling battery supply security should be
   watching solar demand, not EV demand — it sets the price and the queue.
