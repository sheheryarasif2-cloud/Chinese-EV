# Parliament wants lead-acid EV makers shut down — and there is no regulator to do it

**Recorded 28-Aug-2026.** Source: **National Assembly Standing Committee on Industries and
Production**, proceedings reported July 2026 (Dawn, ARY, Profit, ProPakistani, TechJuice —
consistent across five outlets). Grade: <span>press reporting of a parliamentary committee, not the
committee's own minutes.</span>

---

## 🔴 The recommendation

> **"The companies using lead-acid batteries in electric motorcycles should be shut down."**
> — **Naz Baloch**, committee member

**Reasons given:** substandard battery quality, and **risk of battery explosions in hot weather.**

⚠️ **No deadline, no company named, and no count of how many of the 34 licensees use lead-acid.**
This is a committee recommendation, **not** a rule. But it is the clearest signal yet of where policy
is heading.

### 🟢 This validates the single most load-bearing judgement in the project

`battery-layer.md` concluded that **lead-acid dies in 8–13 months at fleet duty cycles in Pakistani
heat**, and `fifth-schedule-primary-text.md` found the tariff **excludes lead-acid from the 1%
concession by name** and taxes it at **double** the lithium rate.

**Parliament has now independently arrived at the same place, for the same reason — heat.**
🟢 **Policy, tariff and engineering now all point the same way.** A lithium-only position is not a
preference in this market; it is the direction of travel.

## 🔴 But the machinery to enforce anything does not exist

| Gap | What the committee found |
|---|---|
| **No regulator** | **There is no dedicated authority for e-motorcycles.** Licences → **EDB** · standards → **PSQCA** · charging → **NEECA** · road regulation → **provincial**. Chair Dr Mehreen Bhutto: *"Too many ministries and departments were involved"* and *"The ministries need to come on the same page."* |
| **No battery policy** | None exists. The **EDB CEO said one was "being finalised."** |
| **Licences don't require lithium** | 🔴 **EDB licences do not mandate lithium-ion**, so licensees may fit cheaper lead-acid and remain compliant |
| **No testing labs** | PSQCA holds **172 standards for electric motorcycles** but has **no dedicated lithium battery testing laboratories**, and *"no effective mechanism to ensure implementation"* |
| **Officials' own words** | Pakistan *"lacks proper testing facilities"* and the issue is **implementing existing standards, not writing new ones** |
| **No complaint channel** | A manufacturer asked: *"If we want to report illegal battery manufacturers, is there any forum where we can lodge such a complaint?"* — **the question went unanswered** |

🔴 **So the PSQCA release certificate found on the battery tariff line in TIPP is a paper gate
without laboratory capacity behind it.** That cuts both ways: it is **easier to clear than feared**,
and **worth less than it appears** as a mark of quality or a barrier to competitors.

## 🔴 The grey market — and it lands directly on Route B

> **"High import duties fuel illegal refurbishment operations. Unlicensed operators import
> lithium-ion cells as scrap and resell them without registration or quality control, undermining
> legitimate manufacturers."**

**Read that against the tariff findings.** `fifth-schedule-primary-text.md` identified **Route B —
lithium-ion cells at 0% duty** (Part-III serial 143) — as the most attractive entry, needing only
Sales Tax registration and an IOCO quota.

🔴 **There is already an informal channel bringing the same cells in as scrap, at no duty and no
compliance cost at all.** A legitimate importer on Route B pays 0% duty too — but also carries
IOCO quota administration, PSQCA clearance, Sales Tax registration and quality liability, and then
**competes on price against product that carries none of it.**

⚠️ **This is the first evidence found of an informal-sector competitor**, and it attacks the exact
component that is **53% of FOB cost.** It does not kill Route B — a fleet operator selling uptime
rather than loose batteries is far less exposed than a battery retailer — but **any plan to sell
packs into the aftermarket now has a named competitor with a structural cost advantage.**

## And a figure worth keeping

The committee heard that substandard batteries have **2–3 year lifespans and cost PKR 90,000 to
replace.**

🟢 **That is consistent with this project's own numbers** — `battery-import-cost.md` found Pakistani
retail at **PKR 115,000** (discounted from 150,000) for a 72V 30Ah LiFePO4 pack. Same order of
magnitude, from an entirely independent source.

## What this does to the decision

🟢 **Three things get better:**
1. **The lithium-only stance is now the policy direction**, not a contrarian call.
2. **The certification gate is lower than feared** — no testing labs means PSQCA cannot be a hard
   barrier today.
3. **Enforcement arriving later favours a compliant operator.** Anyone building on certified lithium
   is positioned for the moment the battery policy lands; the lead-acid assemblers are not.

🔴 **Three things get worse:**
1. **Regulatory change risk is live and near.** A battery policy is *"being finalised"* — rules could
   change during an entry, and India's shakeout shows what a policy change does to entrants.
2. **A grey market already competes on the cell layer** with no duty and no compliance cost.
3. **There is no regulator to appeal to** — no forum even to report an illegal competitor, on the
   committee's own record.
