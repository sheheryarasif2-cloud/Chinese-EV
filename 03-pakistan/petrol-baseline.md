**Section E of the research plan. Recorded 26-Aug-2026.**

## 🔴 The finding: this is not a competitive market, it is a near-monopoly — and it has already moved

**Atlas Honda sold ~1.69 million motorcycles in FY26, roughly 85–88% of the entire Pakistani
market.** Not the leader. Effectively the market.

Its network, as published:

| Type | Count |
|---|---|
| **3S** — sales, service *and* spare parts | 38 |
| **2S** — service and spare parts | 20 |
| **1S** — spare parts | 5 |
| **Sales dealers** | **500+** |

Production capacity is stated at over 1.5 million units a year. Product ladder: 70cc, 100cc,
125cc, 150cc.

### 🔴 And Atlas Honda has already launched an electric scooter

It has **officially launched the "Icon e" electric scooter** alongside the CG150. More telling
than the launch is the stated posture: **no additional investment is planned for EV production,
because existing facilities are considered sufficient for expected EV demand.**

**Read that against section D.** In India, the winners were the legacy petrol manufacturers who
converted existing plants and existing dealer networks — TVS, Bajaj, Hero took 95.6% of
incremental growth while the best-funded pure-play collapsed on service. **Atlas Honda is
running that exact playbook in Pakistan, now, at near-zero incremental capital.**

### What this does to K11

**K11 asks what stops a larger player taking the position. The answer is now specific:
nothing.** An incumbent with ~88% share, 500+ dealers, 58 service-and-parts points, an amortised
factory and 60 years of brand trust has entered the segment without spending anything material.

A new entrant at PKR 50m would begin with **zero** dealers and **zero** service points against
that. India already ran this experiment, and the pure-play with 50% share and vastly more
capital than PKR 50m lost it on parts availability.

🔴 **This does not automatically kill the project — but it kills "sell electric bikes to
Pakistani consumers through a new brand" as a thesis.** Anything that survives has to be
something Atlas Honda cannot or will not do: fleet supply, battery services, swapping
infrastructure, parts and service for the installed base, or a segment beneath its attention.

## The benchmark product

| | Honda CD 70 (2026) |
|---|---|
| **Retail price** | **PKR 159,900** — up PKR 2,000 on a new tax on imported motorcycle engines in the 2025-26 budget |
| Real-world economy | **55–60 km/l** (54.8 km/l dense city; 67.4 km/l highway at 45 km/h) |
| Running cost | **~PKR 5.85–6.10 per km** at current petrol |

**Petrol: PKR 343.10 per litre as at 25 August 2026** — and volatile; it was PKR 331.95 on
4 August, a 3.4% move inside three weeks.

⚠️ **Fuel price volatility cuts both ways.** It is the single strongest driver of electric
adoption *and* it means the economic case is re-priced every fortnight. **Any TCO model must be
run at several petrol prices, not one.**

## The market it sits in

- **FY26 total motorcycle sales: ~1.93 million units** — an all-time high.
- **H1 2026: ~1.1 million units, +33.8% YoY.**
- Other players: United, Road Prince, Yamaha, Suzuki, Plum Qingqi, Ravi — sharing roughly 12–15%
  between them.

🔴 **Road Prince matters disproportionately** — it is Yadea's Pakistani partner, so one of the
few non-Honda networks is already carrying the world's largest e-2W brand.

# 🟢 RESOLVED 27-Aug-2026 — the PKR 343.10 figure is correct

The conflict flagged earlier (PKR 343.10 verified against a press figure of PKR 414–415) is
closed. **PKR 343.10 is right; the 414–415 figure is wrong** and should not be used.

Verified across multiple independent dates in August 2026:

| Effective | PKR/litre | Move |
|---|---:|---|
| 7 Aug 2026 | 329.82 | −3.19 |
| 21 Aug 2026 | 337.78 | +0.27 |
| 22 Aug 2026 | 341.59 | — |
| **26–27 Aug 2026** | **343.10** | **+1.12 from 341.98** |

Petroleum Division notification, reported consistently by ARY News, Daily Pakistan and
Mashriq TV. **The whole month sits in a 329.82–343.10 band. Nothing approaches 414.**

## 🔴 The more important discovery: Pakistan now reprices fuel DAILY

**Since August 2026 petrol prices are reviewed daily under a new OGRA pricing mechanism**, with
revised rates published each day — the 26-Aug notification was explicitly valid only *"until
August 27, 2026."*

This replaces the fortnightly regime every earlier assumption in this research was built on.

**What it changes:**

1. ✅ **The TCO model is unaffected in direction.** Petrol moved 4.0% across the month; the
   electric running-cost advantage is 3–7×. **No plausible daily move reverses the sign.**
2. 🔴 **But no petrol figure in this research can be quoted as "the price" again** — only as
   "the price on a stated date." Every cost-per-km number must now carry its date.
3. 🟢 **It strengthens the fleet pitch and weakens the consumer one.** A rider facing a price
   that can move every morning values a fixed electricity cost more, not less. **Daily
   repricing makes fuel volatility a felt weekly experience rather than a fortnightly news
   event** — and volatility, not just level, is what an electric bike removes.
4. ⚠️ **It is also a downside risk.** A sustained fall in global crude now transmits to the
   pump within days rather than weeks, eroding the saving faster than under the old regime.
   **The TCO case should be re-run at a lower petrol price as a sensitivity** —
   `[PLACEHOLDER: TCO at PKR 300 and PKR 275/litre]`.
