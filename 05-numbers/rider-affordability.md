# Rider affordability — solved

**Recorded 26-Aug-2026.** The open commercial question was: a rider earning PKR 35,000/month
cannot produce PKR 248,500 upfront, so an excellent payback is irrelevant.

**It is solvable, and the solution is better than a sale.**

---

## 🔴 The insight: the fuel saving *is* the payment capacity

The rider does not need savings. They need a payment smaller than what they already spend.

| | PKR/month |
|---|---:|
| Petrol today | **17,841** |
| Electricity for the same distance | 1,736 |
| **Headroom created** | **16,105** — or **PKR 619 per working day** |

**Any charge below PKR 619/day leaves the rider better off from the first day**, with no deposit,
no savings and no credit history. The asset pays for itself out of a cost the rider is already
bearing.

This reframes the problem entirely: **it was never an affordability problem, it was a
financing-structure problem.**

## The model: daily rental, not sale

| Rate/day | Rider keeps/month | We collect/month | Payback @ PKR 150k bike |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 | 8,305 | 7,800 | 19.2 mo |
| **350** | **7,005** | **9,100** | **16.5 mo** |
| 400 | 5,705 | 10,400 | 14.4 mo |
| 450 | 4,405 | 11,700 | 12.8 mo |

At **PKR 350/day the rider is PKR 7,005/month better off** — and stops buying petrol, stops
paying for engine servicing, and carries no asset risk. We collect PKR 9,100/month per machine.

### Rent-to-own for retention

24 months at PKR 10,000/month collects **PKR 240,000** and hands the rider ownership. They are
still **PKR 6,105/month better off throughout** — and they have a reason not to churn.

---

## 🟢 This is a proven model, not a theory

**Zypp Electric (India)** runs exactly this:

- **₹100–300 per day** rental to delivery riders
- **52-week commitment converts to ownership** — rent-to-own
- **20,000+ EVs deployed**, serving Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, Uber, Rapido, Porter and
  BigBasket riders
- **US$76.5m raised** — ENEOS, Gogoro, Anthill Ventures

The customer set is identical to Pakistan's, the duty cycle is identical, and the affordability
constraint is identical. ⚠️ The ₹ rates do not convert without a dated FX rate and are quoted as
structural evidence, not as a price benchmark.

## 🔴 The collection problem, and the thing that actually solves it

Renting assets to informal workers is a credit-risk business, and that is where this model
usually dies: default, disappearance, disputes.

**The fix is not to collect from the rider. Collect at the platform.**

If foodpanda or Bykea deducts the daily rate from rider earnings **before payout**, the exposure
stops being thousands of informal-worker credit risks and becomes **a single B2B receivable from
a funded company.** That is a completely different risk profile, and it is the difference between
a lending business and a supply business.

Supporting controls: GPS and remote immobiliser, a modest deposit, and starting only with riders
already active on a platform rather than the open market.

⚠️ **This requires the platform to agree, and no platform has been approached.** It is the single
most important conversation in the entire project — ahead of suppliers, ahead of banks.

## 🔴 A discovery that changes the arithmetic

Punjab's E-Bike Scheme 2026 offers bikes at **PKR 15,000 down plus PKR 2,000–3,000/month over 24
months** — a total of roughly **PKR 63,000–87,000**, through Bank of Punjab at zero interest with
the government paying the markup, targeting **100,000 bikes**.

**Those are not PKR 248,500 machines.** Even allowing for heavy subsidy, the scheme implies a
substantial market segment of electric bikes priced far below the Yadea T5 this project has been
modelling against.

**If a fit-for-purpose fleet bike costs PKR 120,000–150,000 rather than 248,500, everything
improves:**

| Bike cost | Bikes for PKR 50m | Gross revenue @ PKR 350/day |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 248,500 | 201 | PKR 21.9m/yr |
| 180,000 | 277 | PKR 30.2m/yr |
| **150,000** | **333** | **PKR 36.4m/yr** |
| 120,000 | 416 | PKR 45.4m/yr |

🔴 **Open item: price and assess the cheap end of the Pakistani market.** The project has been
anchored on a premium product. That may have been the wrong benchmark all along, and it is a
cheap thing to check.

⚠️ Note also what the scheme is *not*: it is **students only**, one per family, allocated by
ballot. **Delivery riders are not eligible.** It proves the state will fund markup on e-bikes; it
does not finance this business.

## Staged, so capital is not locked before the model is proven

**Phase 1 — sell outright to fleet operators.** Capital-light, fast cycle, lowest risk. Proves the
product, the service capability and the supply chain without financing anyone. Lower margin,
accepted deliberately.

**Phase 2 — rental with platform-deducted collection.** The core model. Requires Phase 1's service
capability to already exist, because **uptime is the product**.

**Phase 3 — rent-to-own.** Retention and lifetime value, once default behaviour is observed rather
than assumed.

**Battery-as-a-service overlays all three.** Leasing the battery separately keeps the recurring
margin — the 28.4% of revenue that batteries represent in Yadea's audited accounts — and, more
importantly, **removes the rider's single worst outcome: a dead pack they cannot afford to
replace.** It converts K5 from a liability into a product.

## What this does to the risk profile

| | Sale to riders | Rental with platform collection |
|---|---|---|
| Affordability | 🔴 Blocks the deal | 🟢 Solved — payment < existing spend |
| Credit risk | 🔴 Thousands of informal workers | 🟢 One B2B receivable |
| Battery risk | 🔴 Rider stranded, blames brand | 🟢 Ours, priced in, managed |
| Capital | 🟢 Recovered at sale | 🔴 **Locked 13–20 months per machine** |
| Recurring revenue | 🔴 None after sale | 🟢 Rental + battery + service |

**The trade is capital intensity for risk and margin.** That is the honest shape of it: this
becomes an asset-finance business with a supply arm, not a distribution business.
