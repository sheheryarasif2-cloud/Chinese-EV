## 🔴 ELFA is a Wavetec company. That changes the competitive read.

`swapping-and-the-punjab-concentration.md` recorded that **ELFA took Bykea** and treated ELFA as one
more Pakistani assembler from the catalogue. **It is not.**

> **ELFA Electric is a product of EV Technologies, a subsidiary of Wavetec** — a company that has
> *"operated globally for over 37 years"*, with a **30-year R&D legacy** cited in its own materials.

**Wavetec is an established Pakistani-origin multinational**, not a startup. So the competitor
holding the Bykea relationship in Karachi is backed by a **37-year-old global technology business**
with real engineering depth.

⚠️ **Re-read the competitive picture with that in mind:**

| Player | What they are | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas Honda** | ~88% incumbent, 500+ dealers, premium consumer EV | National |
| **Zyp Technologies** | VC-backed, own factory, BaaS swap network | Lahore |
| **ELFA / EV Technologies** | 🔴 **Wavetec subsidiary — 37-year multinational — holds Bykea** | **Karachi** |
| **foodpanda + Wasl** | Platform + SECP-regulated NBFC | Lahore |
| 34 EDB licensees | ~660 units/year each on average | Mostly Punjab |

🔴 **So the Karachi delivery segment — the opening this research kept circling back to — is held by
a Wavetec subsidiary, and the swap-network model is held by a funded manufacturer in Lahore.**
Neither is a weak incumbent. **What is still unknown is scale:** no rider count, unit count or
target has ever been published for the ELFA–Bykea partnership, and Zyp is visibly at ~17% of its own
network plan. **Weak execution is not the same as a weak competitor, and neither has been measured.**

## 🟢 Electricity in Karachi may be CHEAPER than the model assumed

`tco-model.md` uses **PKR 39.70/kWh** (the NEV Policy's commercial charging rate) and **33.38**
(national average). **K-Electric's own published rates sit below both.**

| K-Electric category | PKR/unit | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| **Commercial** | **18.50 – 28.50** | Varies by business category and consumption |
| **Industrial B1** *(small)* | **26.23** | Cut from 30.80, **effective Feb 2026 → Dec 2026** |
| Industrial B2 *(medium)* | 26.16 | Cut from 30.73 |
| Industrial B3 *(high-tension)* | 27.00 | Cut from ~31 |

🔴 **DO NOT drop these straight into the TCO.** The final bill adds **FCA (fuel cost adjustment),
QTA (quarterly tariff adjustment), GST and meter rent** — and in Pakistan those adjustments are not
trivial. **The base rate is not the effective rate.** Commercial tariffs also carry **fixed charges
based on sanctioned load**, which matter a great deal for a swap cabinet that draws hard in bursts.

🟢 **But the direction is favourable and worth stating:** a fleet or swap operator on a commercial or
industrial connection in Karachi is buying electricity at a **published base of PKR 18.50–28.50**,
against the **39.70** the model conservatively assumed. **The economics in the TCO section are, if
anything, understated for a commercial operator.**

⚠️ **Note the expiry: the industrial cuts run only to end-December 2026.**

## The connection itself — process known, cost not

**K-Electric new commercial/industrial connection:** online application → **site survey to assess
load and infrastructure** → **demand note issued (this is where the connection charge appears)** →
meter installed in **15–20 days**.

🔴 **The connection charge is not published — it is quoted per site after survey.** So the cost that
`swapping-and-the-punjab-concentration.md` identified as the real constraint behind a swap point
**cannot be researched; it must be requested per location.** The 15–20 day meter timeline is
however much faster than expected and is not a bottleneck.

## Insurance — a fleet operating cost that was never in the model

| Cover | Annual premium |
|---|---|
| **Third-party** *(legally required)* | **PKR 2,500 – 4,000** for a 70cc bike |
| **Comprehensive** | **PKR 5,000 – 20,000+** depending on value, age and location |

⚠️ **Karachi is explicitly rated higher-risk** for traffic density and accident rates — so a Karachi
fleet sits at the upper end, not the middle.

**What this does to the fleet model:** at a mid-range **PKR 12,000 comprehensive**, a **200-machine
fleet carries roughly PKR 2.4m a year in insurance** — against a PKR 50m capital base. **It is not
fatal, but it was entirely absent from the model**, and for a rental operator comprehensive cover is
not optional the way it might be for an owner-rider.

### 🔴 And the same cc problem appears again

**"Engine capacity is one of the biggest determinants of cost."**

That is the **third** place this research has hit the same structural gap: registration fees are
cc-banded, provincial token tax is cc-banded, **and now insurance premiums are cc-rated** — while an
electric motorcycle has no engine capacity at all.

**Balochistan solved it by publishing a kW→cc conversion** (without publishing the ratio). **No
insurer's approach to rating a zero-cc vehicle has been found.** `[PLACEHOLDER: how an insurer
rates an e-bike]` — **and this is a broker question, not a research question.**

⚠️ **No EV-specific motorcycle insurance product or pricing was found in Pakistan at all.** Whether
insurers will write comprehensive cover on a lithium-battery vehicle at standard rates — given
battery fire risk — **is unestablished and is a real exposure for a rental fleet**, where the
operator owns the asset and carries the liability.
