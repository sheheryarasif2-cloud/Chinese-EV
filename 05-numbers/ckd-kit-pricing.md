## What this does to the entry economics

**A first order of 50 kits at ~PKR 55,000 is PKR 2.75 million.** Against a PKR 50m ceiling, that
is **5.5% of available capital** — and 50 units is a real pilot fleet.

Set the kit cost against Pakistani retail:

| | PKR |
|---|---:|
| **CKD kit, landed cost before duty** | **~51,700 – 62,000** |
| Cheapest Pakistani retail e-bike (Evee Flipper) | 97,000 |
| Pakistani volume band | 150,000 – 250,000 |
| Yadea T5 | 253,500 |
| Honda ICON e: | 419,900 |

🔴 **The gap is very large — and it is the whole business.** Even after customs duty (1% on
EV-specific components, 10–15% non-localised), freight, assembly labour, warranty reserve and
distribution margin, a kit starting near PKR 55,000 has room to land well inside the current
Pakistani price band.

**This is the first evidence that the import stack has real margin in it rather than assumed
margin.**

## 🔴 The question that decides whether any of this is real

**Does the kit include the battery?**

Almost certainly **not**, in most cases. Only one listing mentions batteries explicitly — the
3000W kit *"with CKD and lead-acid batteries"* at **PKR 348,189–354,418**, roughly **six times**
the price of the battery-less kits.

That single comparison implies the battery is the dominant cost, which matches everything else in
this research: Yadea earns **28.4% of revenue from batteries and chargers**, and MS Jaguar charges
**+PKR 71,000** for the LFP version of the same bike.

**So the true landed cost is kit + battery, and the battery may exceed the kit.**
`[PLACEHOLDER: battery cost at import, by chemistry and capacity]` — until that is known, no
landed-cost model is complete, and **the favourable arithmetic above is only half the picture.**

## Other cautions, stated plainly

1. **"CKD kit" has no consistent definition here.** Some listings are complete vehicle kits minus
   battery; some are conversion kits (motor, controller, harness); some are frames. **Each quote
   must specify exactly what is in the box.**
2. **Prices are seller-declared and Alibaba-converted.** The PKR figures use Alibaba's own
   undated rate. A real quote will be in USD or CNY, FOB.
3. **EEC certification appears on several listings** — European conformity, which says nothing
   about Pakistani PSQCA or EDB acceptance.
4. **Nothing here is quality-assured.** Reorder rates of 18–80% are shown on some sellers; that
   is Alibaba's own metric, not an audit.
5. **Nothing here carries a warranty backstop.** This is exactly the tier `audited-financials.md`
   flagged: reachable, and weakest on the thing K5 exists to protect.

## What a real quote must ask for

Any RFQ has to pin down, or the number is meaningless:

1. **Exactly what the kit contains** — itemised, with and without battery.
2. **Battery** — chemistry, voltage, Ah, kWh, cycle rating, and price as a separate line.
3. **FOB price in USD or CNY** at 50, 100 and 200 units, plus the port.
4. **Whether they will supply below container load**, and freight cost either way.
5. **Warranty** — duration, what is covered, and **who honours a claim from Pakistan** (K5).
6. **Certification** — what they hold, and what documentation ships with the goods for EDB.
7. **Spare parts** — availability, pricing, lead time (K8).
8. **Lead time** from deposit, and payment terms — **whether an LC is acceptable** (K4).
