# Research execution doc — Chinese e-two-wheelers into Pakistan

## Context

The workspace already holds primary-sourced work: five audited filings (Yadea, Aima, Xinri,
Luyuan, NIU), the NEV Policy 2025-30, FBR Budget 2026-27, SRO 656(I)/2006, AIDEP 2021-26, and
UN Comtrade trade flows — all in `06-sources/`. What exists is in `02-supply/` and
`03-pakistan/`.

This doc turns an eight-part outline into an executable research brief, **adds what the outline
is missing**, and gives each section its sources, schema and failure conditions.

**Everything here serves one decision: go / no-go on entering the electric two-wheeler business
in Pakistan at roughly PKR 50m of risk capital.** A section that cannot change that decision
does not belong.

---

## Part 1 — What the outline is missing

Fifteen gaps, ordered by how much each could change the answer.

### 🔴 1. Battery economics — the single largest omission
Yadea's audited accounts show **batteries and chargers at 28.4% of revenue**, with **18.3m
batteries sold against 16.3m vehicles**. The outline treats vehicles as the product. In this
industry the battery is the product, the recurring revenue, the main cost, the main safety
risk, and the main warranty liability. Needs its own section: chemistry (lead-acid vs lithium
vs sodium-ion), **swappable vs fixed** as a business-model fork, battery as % of BOM,
replacement cycle, its **separate tariff line**, fire-safety regulation, and who carries the
warranty.

### 🔴 2. The India comparable — empirical proof of the policy risk
India ran a subsidy scheme structurally identical to Pakistan's NEV cost-sharing, and it ended
badly: the SFIO investigated **Hero Electric, Okinawa and Benling** over ~INR 2.97bn in
subsidy claims and ordered refunds of ~INR 469 crore; **Hero Electric and Benling laid off 80%+
of permanent staff**; Hero and Okinawa's combined share fell **from 31% in 2022 to 10%**; and
when FAME-II lapsed, e-2W sales **fell 53% in a single month**.

**This is what K7 looks like when it actually fires, next door, within three years.** Pakistan
is now running a PKR 65,000/unit subsidy that **tapers to 80/60/40%** and sits under a
concession regime that **sunsets 30 June 2027.** No section of this research is more directly
predictive than this one, and the outline omits it entirely.

### 🔴 3. Fleet and B2B — the outline is consumer-only
In China and India the beachhead for electric two-wheelers was **delivery and courier fleets**,
not private buyers, because high daily mileage makes the running-cost saving overwhelming.
Pakistan has a large and growing delivery economy. A fleet channel has different economics
(volume, financing, service contracts, battery swapping) and may be the only route where a
small entrant can win. **Add it as a first-class channel, not a footnote.**

### 🔴 4. Total cost of ownership vs a 70cc — the actual purchase decision
No Pakistani buyer compares an e-bike to another e-bike. They compare it to a Honda CD70.
Needs: purchase price gap, **cost per km** at current petrol prices versus electricity (the NEV
Policy fixes commercial charging at **PKR 39.7/kWh**), battery replacement amortised, service
costs, resale. **Without this the entire demand case is assertion.**

### 5. After-sales, parts and service network
The standard failure mode of imported vehicles, and already a kill criterion (K8). Parts
pipeline, stocking depth, HV-trained technicians, warranty claim rates, service coverage.

### 6. Consumer financing
At PKR 150,000–250,000 against a ~PKR 150,000 petrol bike, **financing decides adoption more
than sticker price.** Bank auto-finance appetite for e-bikes, the approved interest-free loan
scheme, instalment and BNPL models, and what residual-value uncertainty does to loan terms.

### 7. Certification beyond EDB
EDB certification is documented in `03-pakistan/edb-certification.md`, but not: **PSQCA
standards, type approval, the vehicle class an e-bike registers under**, and the WP.29 safety
regulations AIDEP commits to.

### 8. Unit economics and working capital
Landed cost is the first line of a P&L, not the answer. Needs the full stack — landed → dealer
margin → retail — plus **breakeven volume, inventory days, receivable days, the cash conversion
cycle, and return on the PKR 50m.** An import business dies of working capital, not of margin.

### 9. Risk register, with FX first
**PKR volatility is an existential risk to an import business** and is absent from the outline.
Plus policy risk (quantified by the India section), supplier concentration, warranty tail,
battery fire liability, and demand risk.

### 10. Competitive response
Entry assumes incumbents stand still. Atlas Honda dominates Pakistani two-wheelers; what does
it do when e-bikes reach 10% share? What happens when Chinese brands come direct, as Yadea has
via Road Prince?

### 11. Legal and corporate setup
**Sales Tax Act 1990 registration is a hard prerequisite for SRO 656 concessions** — that alone
makes this a research item, not an afterthought. Plus entity type, NTN, import licence, WeBOC
registration.

### 12. Go-to-market and distribution
NIU runs 4,540 franchised stores in China to sell 1.1m units. What does Pakistan actually
require — own retail, dealer appointment, online? And **which city first**?

### 13. Residual value and the used market
Drives financing terms and buyer confidence. Chronically weak for new EV brands.

### 14. Decision calendar
The **30 June 2027 concession sunset** creates a clock. Work backwards: when must EDB
certification start, when must an order be placed, what must be true by when.

### 15. Pre-mortem
Assume it is 2028 and the business failed. Write the three most likely causes now, while
judgement is uncommitted.

## Part 3 — The prompting doc

Standing rules apply to every section; they are in Part 4.

### A · China supply — the 200+ list
> Build a manufacturer register of 200+ Chinese electric two-wheeler makers, **tiered by
> evidence**, never flat. **Tier 1:** listed companies with audited filings (Yadea 1585.HK,
> Aima 603529.SH, Xinri 603787.SH, Luyuan 2451.HK, NIU, Segway-Ninebot 689009.SH, VMoto) —
> revenue, net margin, unit volumes by product, overseas share, plants. **Tier 2:** large
> unlisted groups (Huaihai, Zongshen, Loncin, Tailg, CFMOTO) from company materials. **Tier 3:**
> export-facing ODM and trading houses from B2B storefronts — name, location, MOQ, CKD/SKD
> availability, certifications; **self-declared, never a volume source.**
>
> Per company: legal name (EN + 中文), brands, entity type, HQ, founded, listing + ticker,
> parent/subsidiary, production capacity, plants incl. overseas, product segments, battery
> types, **MOQ**, CKD/SKD availability, export markets, South Asia presence, certifications,
> published business contact.
>
> **Explain the trend, don't just tabulate it.** Why exports now? The November 2024 national
> standard on batteries and chargers, the 2025 trade-in policy, the lead-acid-to-lithium
> forced replacement cycle, and a maturing domestic market (~49.6m units in 2024 against a
> ~400m installed base). **Reconcile or flag** the contradiction already found: association
> rankings cannot be squared with audited filings — Aima filed 2,615,982 e-motorcycles yet is
> absent from a "3,506,200-unit total market" table.

### B · Battery layer 🆕
> Answer, with sources: lead-acid vs lithium vs sodium-ion — cost, cycle life, weight, heat
> tolerance (**Pakistani summers are the binding constraint**), and what is actually being
> exported. **Battery as % of vehicle BOM and of retail price.** Replacement interval and cost
> — the single largest ownership expense after purchase. **Swappable vs fixed:** capital
> profile, who owns the battery, whether Pakistan's planned two/three-wheeler swapping stations
> change the model. **The separate tariff line** — batteries are not the vehicle's HS code, and
> lead-acid is treated differently from lithium in the EV concession. Fire safety and any
> Pakistani standard. **Who carries the battery warranty in a CKD arrangement** — this is K5.

### C · Global market
> Total e-2W market size and growth. **Chinese export volume and value by destination** — 2024
> was 22m+ units and US$5.8bn, with Europe/Asia/North America ~90% of value. Country-by-country
> adoption: what drove it (subsidy, fuel price, restriction policy, congestion), what the
> penetration curve looked like, and **what happened when support was withdrawn.** Pull
> China→Pakistan and India/Vietnam/Indonesia/Bangladesh comparators from the `un-comtrade`
> connector. 🔴 **Comtrade output must not be republished** — licence prohibits redistribution.

### D · The India case study 🆕
> The most transferable evidence available. Cover: FAME-II design and how closely it matches
> Pakistan's NEV cost-sharing; **what happened at expiry** (e-2W sales −53% in a month;
> beneficiary two-wheelers cut from ~989,000 to under 564,000); **the compliance blow-up** —
> SFIO action against Hero Electric, Okinawa and Benling over ~INR 2.97bn, refunds of ~INR 469
> crore ordered, 80%+ layoffs, share collapsing from 31% to 10%; who survived and why (Ola,
> Ather, TVS, Bajaj) and what they had that the casualties did not.
>
> **Then state the transferable lessons explicitly**, including: a subsidy-dependent model is a
> policy bet; **localisation-claim compliance carries clawback risk**; and share built on price
> support evaporates when support does.

### E · Pakistan petrol baseline
> Total two-wheeler fleet and annual sales (FY26 ~1.93m units, all-time high; H1 2026 ~1.1m,
> +33.8%). **Brand market share** — Atlas Honda, United, Road Prince, Yamaha, Suzuki, Qingqi,
> Ravi. 🔴 Sourced from **PAMA's own data files, not press** — and note PAMA counts members
> only, which is exactly why the earlier "8–9% penetration" figure was withdrawn
> (`03-pakistan/market-data-and-its-limits.md`). The 70cc price point, running cost and
> ownership pattern are the benchmark everything else is measured against.

### F · Pakistan EV landscape — the company table
> Per company: legal name, brand(s), ownership/parent, HQ and plant location, **EDB licence
> status**, installed capacity, models with prices, units sold, battery type, distribution
> footprint, service network, Chinese partner if any, funding.
>
> Covers at least: Jolta, Vlektra, MS Jaguar (MS Group), Sazgar, Road Prince (Yadea partner),
> Okla, Crown, United Auto, Metro, ELFA, Pakzon, EcoDost. 🔴 **Reconcile against the EDB licence
> register** — press counts range from 31 to "50+", with reportedly only ~6 manufacturing. **A
> licence is not a factory; record which are actually producing.** Cross-check the claim that
> Pakistan holds ~2m units/year of "heavily underutilized" e-2W/3W capacity — **whose is it?**

### G · Demand 🆕 in part
> **TCO model vs a 70cc**, the centrepiece: purchase price, cost per km (petrol vs electricity
> at PKR 39.7/kWh commercial), battery replacement amortised, service, insurance, resale.
> **Charging reality:** home charging access by housing type, load-shedding, and — given
> Sheheryar's solar background — whether solar charging is a genuine differentiator here.
> **Cultural and practical preferences:** pillion and family use, load carrying, range anxiety
> against real trip lengths, styling (scooter vs motorcycle form), women riders. **Fleet/B2B:**
> delivery and courier operators, fleet size, duty cycles, and whether fleet economics work
> earlier than consumer. **Five-year forecast**, stated as scenarios with explicit assumptions,
> and **always run once with no subsidy at all.**

### H · Regulatory stack
> Consolidate `03-pakistan/*` and extend: **PSQCA standards, type approval, registration class
> for e-bikes**, WP.29 commitments. **Corporate setup** — entity, NTN, **Sales Tax Act 1990
> registration (a hard prerequisite for SRO 656)**, import licence, WeBOC. Provincial
> incentives. 🔴 **Every rate re-checked against the current schedule** — the concessions sunset
> **30 June 2027** and AIDEP 2026-31 is still an unapproved draft.

### I · Product variants
> What China builds, what sells in comparable markets, what Pakistan's roads, loads and climate
> actually need. Compare on: form factor, motor power, battery capacity and chemistry, range,
> load rating, ground clearance, water/heat tolerance, spare-part commonality.
> **Then recommend one variant to launch first, and defend it** on TCO against a 70cc, parts
> availability, EDB certifiability and MOQ reachability — not on specification.

### J · Pricing and landed cost
> **China price list** — ex-works by model and tier, anchored to audited ASPs already derived:
> **RMB 1,415 per electric bicycle, RMB 1,878 per electric scooter** (Yadea FY2025).
> **Pakistan retail price list** — every local brand and model.
> **Landed-cost model for CKD**, built line by line: FOB → freight → insurance → **customs duty
> (1% EV-specific components / 10–15% non-localised, PCT 8711.6040)** → additional customs duty
> → regulatory duty → sales tax → withholding → clearing → inland freight → assembly cost →
> warranty reserve → dealer margin → retail.
> 🔴 **Build it twice — with concessions and with none.** 🔴 **Every FX rate carries its date.**
> Deliver as a spreadsheet with assumptions on their own tab, so a rate change re-runs it.

### K · Unit economics 🆕
> Full P&L per unit and per year. **Breakeven volume.** Working capital: inventory days,
> LC terms, receivables, the cash conversion cycle. **Peak funding requirement against the PKR
> 50m ceiling** — noting that K8's PKR 7.5m parts-and-training ceiling leaves ~PKR 42.5m.
> Return and payback. Sensitivity on FX, duty, volume and warranty rate. **Which single
> variable breaks it first.**

### L · Channel and go-to-market 🆕
> Distribution options and what each costs. Consumer financing — bank appetite, the
> interest-free scheme, instalments. After-sales: parts depth, technician training, warranty
> process, service coverage (K8). **Which city first, and why.** Fleet channel as a possible
> beachhead.

### M · Competition and risk 🆕
> Incumbent response, direct Chinese entry, price-war exposure. Then a risk register scoring
> likelihood × impact × mitigation, led by **FX volatility**, then policy withdrawal (evidenced
> by section D), supplier concentration, warranty tail, battery fire liability, demand.

### N · Decision calendar and pre-mortem 🆕
> Work backwards from **30 June 2027**: what must be true by when — EDB engagement, supplier
> selection, order placement, first units. Then the pre-mortem: it is 2028 and this failed —
> the three most likely causes, written now.

### O · Executive summary and recommendation
> Written **last**, and only from what the sections established. Answer the actual question —
> go, no-go, or go in a narrowed form — name the entry mode, state the capital, and **list what
> would change the answer.** 🔴 **Desk research cannot close K3, K4 or K5.** If those remain
> open, the honest output is a **defensible narrowing plus the questions that settle each gate**
> — not a verdict it has not earned.

## Part 4 — Standing rules

From `CLAUDE.md`; restated because they are the whole discipline.

- **Every figure carries a source and a date**, saved to `06-sources/`. Search summaries are
  leads, not sources.
- **Evidence grade on every number:** audited / association / company-declared / press /
  self-declared / unknown. **Audited beats everything; where they conflict, the filing wins.**
- **CNY, PKR and USD each labelled; nothing silently converted.** No FX conversion without a
  rate and a date.
- **Never average mirror statistics** — record both and say which is better evidenced.
- **Count the manufacturing entity, not the badge.** Known traps: Xinri = Sunra · Tailing ≈
  Tailg · Aima / "Emma" · ZEEHO is CFMOTO's brand · Segway = Ninebot · **Zongshen and Huaihai
  are entangled.**
- `[PLACEHOLDER]` where a number is genuinely unknown. **Never estimate to fill a cell.**
- 🔴 **UN Comtrade data must never be published** — local use only.
- **Model every case twice: with concessions and without.**

## Part 5 — Order of work

Evidence before conclusions; cheapest kills first.

1. **D (India)** — first, deliberately. It is the cheapest section and it may reframe everything.
2. **B (battery)** and **E (petrol baseline)** — both feed the TCO model.
3. **G (TCO)** — the demand case is assertion until this exists.
4. **A**, **C**, **F** — the register work, parallelisable.
5. **H** (regulatory consolidation), then **I** (variant pick).
6. **J** → **K** — pricing feeds unit economics; neither works before H confirms rates.
7. **L**, **M**, **N**.
8. **O** last.

## Part 7 — What this cannot deliver

**No supplier has been contacted.** K3 (will anyone supply you), K4 (can you open an LC now)
and K5 (is there an enforceable battery backstop) are answerable only by people. The EDB
questions are already scripted in `01-question/edb-call-script.md`.

**A 200+ list will be mostly Tier 3** — self-declared B2B listings. That is acceptable as a
contact register and unacceptable as evidence of scale. The document must say which each row is.

**Nothing here converts to PKR without a dated rate**, so every headline number stays in its
source currency until one is supplied.
