01 — the answer first

Demand is not the risk. Being the seller is.

Four independent lines of evidence converge on one conclusion.

The economics of electric two-wheelers in Pakistan are outstandingly good. They survive the worst domestic electricity tariff, a punishing battery assumption, and the complete removal of every government incentive — simultaneously. That much is settled.

What the evidence supports

A fleet-first entry serving delivery riders, sold on arithmetic. Fuel is 30–71% of a Pakistani delivery rider's gross earnings. An electric bike repays its premium in four to six months with no subsidy. There are 50,000+ known fleet riders, and PKR 50m buys enough machines to need under 1% of that market.

What the evidence does not support

A new consumer brand. Atlas Honda holds ~88% of the market on 1.69m units a year, runs 500+ dealers, and has launched an electric scooter while stating it needs no additional investment to do so. India ran this experiment: the best-funded pure-play fell from over 50% share to 6.8% — beaten on service, not price.

Three gates cannot be closed from a desk

Whether a manufacturer will supply at this scale (K3), whether a letter of credit can be opened today (K4), and whether an enforceable battery warranty backstop exists (K5). RFQs went to five suppliers on 27 August; no reply yet. A fourth gate was added by the research: whether a delivery platform will deduct rental from rider earnings at source.

Coverage boundary, stated plainly

Pakistan and India are near-complete — both have a dominant public aggregator listing every model with a price. China cannot be enumerated the same way: hundreds of manufacturers, thousands of SKUs, brand sites geo-blocked, retail pricing behind Tmall and JD sessions, and heavy white-labelling. The Chinese rows are verified price points and tiers, not an SKU catalogue. Anyone claiming a complete Chinese model list is either scraping a B2B storefront or making it up.

What Pakistan's list shows

  • The entry point is PKR 97,000 — Evee Flipper, well below the CD 70's 159,900.
  • ~35 models cluster within PKR 100,000 of each other. Nothing is differentiated on price.
  • Chemistry is the real price ladder. MS Jaguar's Bolt is 174,000 standard and 245,000 in LFP — +PKR 71,000 for the same machine. REVOO shows +42,000 and +62,000.
  • Yadea spans 174,000 to 1,400,000 — the only brand credibly present at both ends.

What India's list shows that Pakistan's does not

  • Battery capacity is published as standard, and it is 2–3× Pakistan's. Indian mainstream carries 2.5–5.3 kWh; Pakistan's volume import carries 1.8 kWh.
  • Range at the mainstream price point is not close — India 102–212 km, Pakistan 65–90 km.
  • Honda sells a 102 km Activa e in India and a 65 km ICON e: in Pakistan. Same manufacturer, same segment, materially worse product.
  • The next wave is legacy manufacturers — Suzuki, TVS, Honda and Vinfast — not startups.

Even at the most expensive domestic electricity in Pakistan, the electric bike costs about a third of the petrol bike to run. At the national average it is roughly one-seventh. Battery sensitivity: even at PKR 60,000 replaced every 15 months — a deliberately punishing assumption — the electric bike still saves PKR 1.69/km. Battery cost changes the payback period; it never reverses the sign.

The slab-crossing trap — specific to Pakistan

Charging draws roughly 31 kWh a month. Pakistan's protected domestic tariff cuts off at 200 units, and crossing it removes protected status from the entire bill, not just the marginal units. A household at 175–200 units — plausibly exactly where a CD 70 buyer sits — is pushed over.

The consequence is commercial: a naive savings pitch is contradicted by the customer's next electricity bill. It argues for charging away from home, and makes solar charging a genuine differentiator rather than a marketing line.

And fuel is now repriced daily

The petrol figure was checked against a conflicting press number of 414–415 and PKR 343.10 is confirmed correct: the month ran 329.82 → 343.10, a 4.0% band, across four independently reported notification dates. Since August 2026 petrol is reviewed daily under a new OGRA mechanism — so no petrol figure here can be quoted as "the price", only as the price on a stated date.

It cuts both ways. It strengthens the fleet pitch — volatility is what an electric bike removes — and adds a risk: a sustained crude fall now reaches the pump in days rather than weeks.

Rental rate — who keeps what

Monthly, per rider. The rider's share is what remains of the fuel saving after the daily charge.

Rider keeps Operator collects
The collection mechanism is what actually solves it

Renting assets to informal workers is a credit-risk business, and that is usually where this model dies. The fix is to collect at the platform, not from the rider — foodpanda or Bykea deducting the daily rate from earnings before payout. That converts thousands of informal-worker credit exposures into a single business receivable from a funded company.

Proven template: Zypp Electric in India runs exactly this — ₹100–300/day converting to ownership at 52 weeks, 20,000+ vehicles across Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit and Uber riders, on US$76.5m raised. Same duty cycle, same constraint, same customer.

What it trades away

Rental locks capital for 13–20 months per machine where a sale recovers it immediately. In exchange: recurring revenue, the battery margin, and control of uptime. This becomes an asset-finance business with a supply arm, and should be funded as one.

Cycle life replaces calendar life

At 80–128 km/day against a 75–85 km range, batteries cycle 312–500 times a year. Lead-acid, rated 300–400 cycles, therefore lasts 8–13 months in fleet service. Both chemistries pay back in roughly the same time, because lithium's higher cost is offset by its life. So chemistry is not an economic choice here — it is an operational one, and getting it wrong is precisely how India's largest player lost its market.

What that price buys, in context

The most consequential finding for the fleet thesis

At PKR 419,900 the ICON e: is 2.6× a CD 70 and 1.7× a Yadea T5, while offering less range than the T5 (65 km vs 75–85 km).

And it is structurally useless for fleet work. A rider covering 80–128 km a day cannot use a 65 km machine with a fixed battery, a proprietary charger and a six-hour recharge. No swap, no fast charge, no second pack. The incumbent has taken a premium consumer position, not a fleet one — the gap is confirmed open by the only company that could have closed it.

Road Prince matters disproportionately: one of the few substantial non-Honda networks, and it already carries Yadea, the world's largest electric two-wheeler maker.

06 — the comparable market

India already ran this cycle, and it ended badly for entrants

Same subcontinent, same commuter baseline, same price sensitivity, and a subsidy structurally similar to Pakistan's NEV cost-sharing. Pakistan is at the start of that curve.

2022

Generous FAME-II support draws in entrants. Hero Electric and Okinawa hold 31% combined share.

2023–24

SFIO pursues Hero Electric, Okinawa and Benling over ~INR 2.97bn in localisation-linked claims. ~INR 469 crore ordered repaid; 80%+ of permanent staff laid off. Combined share falls 31% → 10%.

2024

FAME-II lapses. Sales fall 53% in a single month. Beneficiary vehicles cut from ~989,000 to under 564,000.

2024–26

Ola Electric falls from >50% share to 6.8%. 10,644 consumer-helpline complaints, warranty claims refused, scooters queued for parts — Goa suspends its vehicle registrations. FY loss ₹1,833 crore.

2026

Registrations up 53–75% with support slashed to ₹5,000/vehicle. TVS 25%, Bajaj 23%, Ather 16%, Hero 11% — those four take 95.6% of incremental growth.

The three transferable lessons

1. Demand survives subsidy withdrawal. Entrants often do not. The market was captured by whoever already had dealers, service and brand trust. In Pakistan that means Atlas Honda — and it has begun.

2. Service is the decisive variable, not price. Ola had capital, product, brand and half the market. It lost on spare parts and service capacity — and a state regulator removed its right to sell.

3. Localisation-linked subsidy claims carry clawback risk. Pakistan's 1% duty concession is gated on EDB certification and annual input reconciliation. Same structural shape. In India, getting that paperwork wrong was company-ending.

Derived from Yadea's audited revenue table across 16.3 million units — ex-works ASP RMB 1,415 per electric bicycle, RMB 1,878 per electric scooter. Order minimums then split the market in two. The tier that deals at 50–200 units is trading houses and small ODMs. Nothing sits in between, and the reachable tier is where a warranty backstop is weakest.

The supplier spread is bigger than the import margin

At the 50-unit tier the same nominal pack is PKR 59,281 from Koyosonic and PKR 113,939 from Hunan CTS. One is 48% below Pakistani retail; the other matches Pakistani retail before a single rupee of freight, duty or tax.

So the arbitrage is not a property of the trade. It is a property of the supplier. Choosing wrong erases the entire margin — which is why listing prices cannot substitute for quotes.

The volume cliff, and the warranty gap

PKR 42,018 at MOQ 500 against PKR 128,668 at 1–49 — 3.1× cheaper at volume. But MOQ 500 is PKR 21m of batteries alone, 42% of the ceiling, before a single vehicle.

Hunan CTS publishes 1,500 cycles at 90% DOD — 3.0–4.8 fleet years. Read conservatively that is three years, not five — and Koyosonic warrants only 1 year below 50Ah, exactly the pack size a two-wheeler uses. A one-year warranty on a three-year asset is the K5 exposure.

The development to watch

Yadea's sodium-ion Q1 and DE3 at CNY 3,299–3,499 — its entry models now use no lithium at all, with 92% capacity at −20 °C and 80% charge in 15 minutes. If sodium reaches export markets it resets the cost floor and removes lithium price and supply risk from the bill of materials. It is the market leader deploying it, not a startup.

09 — primary law

The tariff regime, read in its own words

The FY2026-27 Fifth Schedule is not a separate document — Finance Act 2026 s.3(14) substituted the whole schedule via its Second Schedule. Read from the Gazette of Pakistan, 26 June 2026.

K7 settled in black-letter law — Gazette page 769

"The concession shall be admissible to manufacturers of electric motorcycle on and from the 1st day of July, 2025 till 30th day of June, 2027 subject to certification and quota determination by the Engineering Development Board (EDB)."

The old "five years from 1st July, 2020" wording was replaced outright. About ten months remain — and EDB certification, supplier selection, an LC, a 15–45 day lead time and a 25–35 day sea leg all have to fit inside it. The decision window is materially shorter than the concession window.

A drafting defect that survived a wholesale substitution

8454 is "converters, ladles, ingot moulds and casting machines of a kind used in metallurgy." It is a steel-making vessel. The auto-rickshaw entry two serials above gives Converter as 8502.4000 — electric rotary converters, the sensible code.

So the schedule assigns a foundry code to an electric motorcycle's converter — and the entire schedule was replaced in June 2026 without fixing it. It is not a transcription slip. It is the law. Paperwork exactly this size is what ended Hero Electric and Okinawa in India.

Two findings the primary text produced that no summary showed

Lead-acid is expressly excluded — the words are "batteries other than lead acid", in all three two- and three-wheeler serials. Pakistan's tariff will not subsidise the chemistry that fails in 8–13 months at fleet duty. Policy and engineering point the same way for once.

And the cleanest concession in the whole schedule is the one nobody looked for: a charging station enters at 0% with "Nil" in the condition column. Since uptime is the fleet product, and a 65 km machine on a six-hour charge is useless at an 80–128 km duty cycle, the hardware that answers that problem lands duty-free.

The vehicle in law — there is no low-speed class

Provincial Motor Vehicles Ordinance 1965, read in full

"Motor cycle" has two tests only: fewer than four wheels, and unladen weight not exceeding 900 pounds (408 kg). There is no wattage, kW, cc or speed threshold anywhere, and section 23(1) is unconditional. Every electric two-wheeler in Pakistan is a motor cycle in law.

⚠ This corrects a claim repeated across Pakistani EV vendor sites — that machines below 250 W need no registration. It has no basis in the Ordinance and appears to be Indian rules restated. So the sub-PKR-97,000 tier cannot be unlocked by regulatory arbitrage — it would take amending primary legislation in four provinces.

The nine things each was asked

  • Itemised packing list, with and without battery
  • Battery chemistry, V, Ah, kWh, cycle rating at a stated depth of discharge, priced separately
  • FOB price at 50, 100 and 200 units, naming the port
  • Whether they supply below full container load
  • Crated dimensions and gross weight — the missing freight input
  • Warranty, and who honours a claim made from Pakistan
  • Certification, and what documentation ships for EDB and PSQCA
  • Spare parts availability, pricing, lead time
  • Lead time, payment terms, whether an LC is acceptable

What to watch for, in order

  1. Who honours a warranty claim from Pakistan. K5 — the weakest point in the thesis. Expect vagueness; press for a named entity
  2. Crated dimensions and gross weight. Turns the freight guess into arithmetic
  3. Whether either battery firm quotes cells only. If yes, Route B goes live — 0% duty, no EDB vehicle certification, on 53% of FOB cost
  4. Whether the 2× battery spread survives a real quote
  5. Whether BENLG already has a Pakistani distributor — a yes narrows the field
  6. LC versus T/T in advance. T/T-only changes the whole working-capital profile

A non-reply is also data. If a supplier will not quote 50–200 units, that answers K3.

The tier structure behind all of this

Order minimums split the Chinese market in two. Huaihai — top-five, own OEM arm, existing Pakistan base — quotes 10,000 units a year, roughly twenty times the capital ceiling. The tier that deals at 50–200 units is trading houses and small ODMs: Wuxi MDKA New Energy, Wuxi Legend International, Zhejiang Taiya and the wider B2B layer, with 15–25 day lead times and CKD/SKD openly offered. Nothing sits in between — and the reachable tier is exactly where a warranty backstop is weakest.

13 — the certification gate

The gate behind the rate is written for a different technology

SRO 656(I)/2006 — the operative condition, confirmed from the primary text

The 1% is not automatic. It requires certification and quota determination by the Engineering Development Board, measured against SRO 656(I)/2006. That instrument contains no electric-vehicle provisions at all — its only use of the word "electric" describes a crane hoist.

The motorcycle schedule an e-bike assembler is judged against mandates an engine assembly line — crank cases, crankshaft and connecting rod, spark plug and tappet adjustment, oil filling — plus an emission tester at final inspection. An electric motorcycle has none of those. Certification therefore rests on how EDB applies an obsolete schedule; eligibility, cost and timeline cannot be self-assessed before applying.

Also required, and not optional

  • Sales Tax Act 1990 registration — a hard prerequisite
  • On-site facility verification
  • Component lists approved by EDB or IOCO and uploaded to WeBOC
  • Annual input reconciliation by 15 August, failing which the manufacturing certificate is not revalidated
  • PSQCA release certificate at import — samples may be lab-tested, and non-conforming shipments are destroyed

The incentive layer

  • NEV subsidy: PKR 65,000 per two-wheeler, tapering to 80/60/40% in years 3, 4 and 5
  • Commercial charging fixed at PKR 39.7/kWh under the NEV Policy
  • PAVE scheme — subsidised financing for e-bikes and e-rickshaws
  • Sales tax on locally manufactured EVs held at 1% (Eighth Schedule)
  • ⚠ Installed local capacity is ~2 million units/yr and "heavily underutilized" — the government's own words. That is the single strongest argument against adding assembly capacity
One on-ramp, status unconfirmed

AIDEP allowed 10 built-up units per variant at half duty, to a maximum 200 units per company, with manufacturing compulsory within two years. AIDEP expired 30 June 2026 and it is unconfirmed whether the two-wheeler pilot continued. It is the most useful provision found in the whole regime, and it may be extinct.

15 — what this does not establish

The honest boundary

  • Five supplier RFQs were sent on 27 August 2026 — three CKD kit makers and both battery suppliers — but no reply has been received, so every supplier figure here remains a published listing, not a quote. No bank, delivery platform or regulator has been contacted at all.
  • Whether a delivery platform will deduct rental at source. The fleet model's collection mechanism depends on it entirely.
  • How EDB applies an ICE-era facility schedule to an electric vehicle. SRO 656(I)/2006 contains no EV provisions at all — its only use of "electric" describes a crane hoist — yet the primary text confirms it as the operative condition. Its motorcycle schedule mandates an engine assembly line and an emission tester. An electric motorcycle has neither.
  • The kW→cc conversion factor. Balochistan names the mechanism without publishing the ratio, and it decides 1% against 2% of vehicle value.
  • Units per container, and dangerous-goods freight on lithium. Freight per unit is currently a container rate divided by an assumption.
  • The IOCO quota process for the 0% battery routes — timeline, cost, feasibility — is entirely unknown. IOCO is a different body from EDB.
  • What a charging or swap station costs. Import duty is 0% and unconditional, but 8504.4030 is a static converter line and a swap cabinet may classify elsewhere. Nothing here covers the K-Electric connection, where the real cost and delay usually sit.
  • Pakistani market volumes are press-reported. PAMA counts members only, which excludes almost every electric maker. A penetration figure computed on that basis was withdrawn from this research.
  • No Pakistani brand publishes unit sales. The catalogues show what is offered, not what sells.
  • Three referenced instruments have not been read: SRO 693(I)/2006, SRO 655(I)/2006, and SRO 929(I)/2024 sr.3(xi) — whose own record in the government portal is self-contradictory on its expiry.
One methodological note worth keeping

Several conclusions in this research were reversed by primary sources after being drawn from trade press or official summaries — a market-share ranking that could not be reconciled with audited filings; a penetration figure computed across mismatched populations; a "10–15%" duty rate that the schedule states flat at 15%; a concession expiry that the printed schedule appeared to have missed. Where a filing or a statute disagreed with a summary, the primary source won every time. Any figure still carrying a Press grade should be treated as provisional.
