01 — the answer first

Demand is not the risk. Being the seller is.

Four independent lines of evidence converge on one conclusion.

The economics of electric two-wheelers in Pakistan are outstandingly good. They survive the worst domestic electricity tariff, a punishing battery assumption, and the complete removal of every government incentive — simultaneously. That much is settled.

What the evidence supports

A fleet-first entry serving delivery riders, sold on arithmetic. Fuel is 30–71% of a Pakistani delivery rider's gross earnings. An electric bike repays its premium in four to six months with no subsidy. There are 50,000+ known fleet riders, and PKR 50m buys enough machines to need under 1% of that market.

What the evidence does not support

A new consumer brand. Atlas Honda holds ~88% of the market on 1.69m units a year, runs 500+ dealers, and has launched an electric scooter while stating it needs no additional investment to do so. India ran this experiment: the best-funded pure-play fell from over 50% share to 6.8% — beaten on service, not price.

Two late findings that change the shape of this, not the sign

The state is subsidising 116,000 machines this fiscal year against a national production of 22,404 — a programme 5.2× the entire industry, at PKR 80,000 a unit. Demand is not merely good; it is being manufactured. And a student in Lahore can already take an e-bike for PKR 2,100 a month, interest-free.

foodpanda has already launched an EV rider fleet with an SECP-regulated NBFC. The platform-plus-financier structure this dossier proposed is not speculative — it is running, in Punjab, with the largest player. Bykea is taken too — ELFA has partnered with it since July 2025. What is genuinely open is Karachi's infrastructure: every swap network, subsidy scheme and donor programme found is in Punjab or Islamabad, and Sindh publishes no EV concession at all. See sections 05–07.

Three gates cannot be closed from a desk

Whether a manufacturer will supply at this scale (K3), whether a letter of credit can be opened today (K4), and whether an enforceable battery warranty backstop exists (K5). RFQs went to five suppliers on 27 August; no reply yet.

Coverage boundary, stated plainly

Pakistan and India are near-complete — both have a dominant public aggregator listing every model with a price. China cannot be enumerated the same way: hundreds of manufacturers, thousands of SKUs, brand sites geo-blocked, retail pricing behind Tmall and JD sessions, and heavy white-labelling. The Chinese rows are verified price points and tiers, not an SKU catalogue. Anyone claiming a complete Chinese model list is either scraping a B2B storefront or making it up.

What Pakistan's list shows

  • The entry point is PKR 97,000 — Evee Flipper, well below the CD 70's 159,900.
  • ~35 models cluster within PKR 100,000 of each other. Nothing is differentiated on price.
  • Chemistry is the real price ladder. MS Jaguar's Bolt is 174,000 standard and 245,000 in LFP — +PKR 71,000 for the same machine. REVOO shows +42,000 and +62,000.
  • Yadea spans 174,000 to 1,400,000 — the only brand credibly present at both ends.

What India's list shows that Pakistan's does not

  • Battery capacity is published as standard, and it is 2–3× Pakistan's. Indian mainstream carries 2.5–5.3 kWh; Pakistan's volume import carries 1.8 kWh.
  • Range at the mainstream price point is not close — India 102–212 km, Pakistan 65–90 km.
  • Honda sells a 102 km Activa e in India and a 65 km ICON e: in Pakistan. Same manufacturer, same segment, materially worse product.
  • The next wave is legacy manufacturers — Suzuki, TVS, Honda and Vinfast — not startups.

There is no single "EV charging rate" — there are three

This dossier models PKR 39.70/kWh, the NEV Policy commercial figure. That is a policy cap, not the tariff a charger pays.

Residential off-peak 23.57 · NEV Policy cap 39.70 · NEPRA EVCS tariff with adjustments 48.78. 🔴 The real charging-station rate is higher than the cap modelled — so for a public charging business the assumption was optimistic.

🟢 For a fleet it runs the other way. A depot charging overnight is not a public station: it buys at a commercial or industrial connection (K-Electric base 18.50–28.50) or at residential off-peak 23.57 — both well below 39.70. And EV charging stations are exempt from certain fuel cost adjustments, which partly answers the FCA caveat raised in the operating-costs section — though whether a private depot qualifies as EVCS is unverified.

The conclusion does not turn on which applies. Even at the worst figure found (48.78) the electric bike costs ~1.22 PKR/km against petrol's 5.72–6.24 — roughly a fifth. Only the margin moves. ⚠ All three figures are press-sourced, not read from a NEPRA determination.

Even at the most expensive domestic electricity in Pakistan, the electric bike costs about a third of the petrol bike to run. At the national average it is roughly one-seventh. This depends only on the price of petrol and the price of a unit of electricity — not on a forecast, a subsidy or a policy.

The slab-crossing trap — specific to Pakistan

Charging draws roughly 31 kWh a month. Pakistan's protected domestic tariff cuts off at 200 units, and crossing it removes protected status from the entire bill, not just the marginal units. A household at 175–200 units — plausibly exactly where a CD 70 buyer sits — is pushed over.

The consequence is commercial: a naive savings pitch is contradicted by the customer's next electricity bill. It argues for charging away from home, and makes solar charging a genuine differentiator rather than a marketing line.

And fuel is now repriced daily

The petrol figure was checked against a conflicting press number of 414–415 and PKR 343.10 is confirmed correct: the month ran 329.82 → 343.10, a 4.0% band, across four independently reported notification dates. Since August 2026 petrol is reviewed daily under a new OGRA mechanism — so no petrol figure here can be quoted as "the price", only as the price on a stated date.

It cuts both ways. It strengthens the fleet pitch — volatility is what an electric bike removes — and adds a risk: a sustained crude fall now reaches the pump in days rather than weeks.

The addressable market is bigger than this dossier assumed — but read it carefully

Pakistan has roughly 1.5 million platform-economy workers, of whom about 0.5 million do "location-based" work — delivery and ride-hailing. Bykea alone reported 500,000+ bikes registered on its platform, though that is a 2020 figure.

⚠ Do not swap 50,000 for 500,000. The 0.5m includes car ride-hailing drivers, not addressable by a two-wheeler fleet; Bykea's 500,000+ is a registration count, not active riders, and six years stale. 50,000+ remains the only figure tied to active delivery riders on named platforms.

🟢 What it changes: a 200-machine fleet needed "under 1% of the known market"; against a location-based workforce of ~500,000 it needs 0.04%. 🔴 What it does not change: the segment is gated by which platform will partner, not by how many riders exist — and both foodpanda and Bykea already have EV partners. A large market behind a closed door is not an opportunity.

Monthly, per rider. The rider's share is what remains of the fuel saving after the daily charge.

Rider keeps Operator collects
The collection mechanism is what actually solves it

Renting assets to informal workers is a credit-risk business, and that is usually where this model dies. The fix is to collect at the platform, not from the rider — foodpanda or Bykea deducting the daily rate from earnings before payout. That converts thousands of informal-worker credit exposures into a single business receivable from a funded company.

Proven template: Zypp Electric in India runs exactly this — ₹100–300/day converting to ownership at 52 weeks, 20,000+ vehicles across Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit and Uber riders, on US$76.5m raised. Same duty cycle, same constraint, same customer.

What it trades away

Rental locks capital for 13–20 months per machine where a sale recovers it immediately. In exchange: recurring revenue, the battery margin, and control of uptime. This becomes an asset-finance business with a supply arm, and should be funded as one.

Cycle life replaces calendar life

At 80–128 km/day against a 75–85 km range, batteries cycle 312–500 times a year. Lead-acid, rated 300–400 cycles, therefore lasts 8–13 months in fleet service. Both chemistries pay back in roughly the same time, because lithium's higher cost is offset by its life. So chemistry is not an economic choice here — it is an operational one, and getting it wrong is precisely how India's largest player lost its market.

Correction to this dossier's own numbers

Earlier sections used PKR 65,000 from the NEV Policy. The operative subsidy under PAVE is PKR 80,000, and subsidised machines are reported to land PKR 50,000–60,000 below comparable market models. The subsidised payback case is therefore better than the 3.9 months modelled — but a commercial offer now competes against a state-backed price floor.

A student in Lahore can take an electric motorcycle for PKR 2,100 a month, interest-free, with zero deposit if female. That is a different customer from a delivery rider — but it is the number any consumer pitch is measured against.

Correction — the PAVE subsidy is two-tier

Earlier versions of this page reported the subsidy as PKR 80,000. The official portal shows PKR 50,000 financed and PKR 80,000 self-finance for e-bikes (200,000 / 400,000 for rickshaws).

🔴 The financed figure is 37.5% lower than reported here — and financed is the relevant case, since the whole premise is that riders cannot pay upfront. The subsidised-payback scenario should be read at PKR 50,000; the 80,000 case applies to cash buyers.

And the supply question is answered

The portal states it plainly: "Only models approved by the Engineering Development Board (EDB) will be offered", shortlisted on safety, performance and compliance.

So PAVE is not an import channel — it is a demand channel routed through EDB-licensed manufacturers, the same 34 companies that produced 22,404 units between them last year.

🟢 For an EDB-certified assembler that is a state-funded order book, and the strongest argument yet for pursuing certification rather than importing built-up units. 🔴 For anyone without it, PAVE is a wall, not a door — the subsidy makes approved models cheaper than an uncertified importer can land. It turns EDB certification from a duty-rate question into a market-access question.

⚠ Eligibility reads as individual — CNIC, age band, female quota — which suggests retail only. Whether a fleet operator can buy PAVE-subsidised units is unverified, and it is a material limit on using the scheme for a rental fleet.

The batch size is the finding

500 units is 2.5× the fleet this dossier modelled and 10× the supplier MOQ — an order of exactly the scale a small entrant could serve, and the tender explicitly leans on existing CKD inventory.

But the window shut in June. Whether further batches follow is the single most valuable question left in this research — and it is a phone call to EDB, not a research task. It also reframes the EDB conversation entirely: not "can I get certified", but "is there a procurement pipeline to sell into".

And the industry this would be entering is sub-scale

34 companies hold an EDB licence for electric two- and three-wheelers, and between them they produced 22,404 units — an average of about 660 each per year. Even allowing for a skewed distribution, that is a workshop, not a factory.

🏆 The good reading

EDB certification is demonstrably passable. The gate this dossier treated as the central unknown has been cleared 34 times. It is not a wall.

⚠ The hard reading

A licence is not a factory, and it is not volume. 660 units a year across 34 licensees says the barrier is not paperwork — it is everything after it.

06 — the uncomfortable one

The fleet thesis is not novel — foodpanda is already doing it

What was found

foodpanda Pakistan has launched an EV delivery fleet, with the Government of Punjab and Wasl Investment Finance, an SECP-regulated NBFC. Launched in Lahore. Riders access EV financing through Wasl.

Wasl runs Wasl Mobility Modaraba and states it provides "cash flow based financing … structured in ways to avoid the need for any form of hard collateral" to micro consumers and small businesses. It had already partnered with YES Electromotive on EV accessibility in 2023.

🏆 It validates the structure

This dossier argued the answer to rider affordability is a platform-plus-financier arrangement collecting against rider earnings rather than lending to individuals. A delivery platform and an NBFC have independently built exactly that. The model is not speculative.

⚠ And it removes first-mover advantage

The platform relationship is the scarcest asset in the whole plan, and in Punjab it is already taken — by the largest player, with government backing.

Three things are not established, and they decide how much this matters

1. Whether it is financing-to-own or a rental fleet. Reporting says riders "access EV financing", which reads as lease-to-ownership — not an operator-owned fleet earning rental. Those are different businesses with different balance sheets.

2. Whether it reaches Karachi. Everything reported is Lahore and Punjab, alongside a Punjab government partnership that has no Sindh equivalent.

3. Scale. No unit count, no target and no terms were disclosed in any report found.

For a Karachi fleet, weatherproof is the specification — and that is the top band

The coming PSQCA standard requires resistance to flooding and monsoon conditions, and Karachi floods annually. So IP-65 is not an upgrade, it is the spec — and it puts both cheap packs below their own customs floor.

Koyosonic assessed at $226.80 against a $213.32 invoice — a 6.3% uplift in the tax base. Dongguan at $169.92 against $151.20 — 12.4%. Duty is 1% on the CKD route and 0% on Route B cells, but sales tax at 18% and withholding at 11–12% are charged on the assessed value, so the uplift flows straight into those.

⚠ It also erodes the cheap-supplier advantage this dossier leaned on. The 2× spread against Hunan CTS survives in absolute terms, but a quote below the floor buys nothing at the border.

And it is aimed squarely at the grey market

The ruling's own background cites "massive under-invoicing … by certain importers", and records that transaction value was inapplicable because "declared values did not correspond to market prices." The valuation floor removes the under-declaration advantage regardless of duty rate, because sales tax and withholding are assessed on the floor. That strengthens the case that a legitimate Route B operator out-competes the scrap trade.

⚠ And a trade-press summary of this ruling says the values are "per kilowatt, not per kg." That is wrong — the ruling PDF shows both columns under "whichever is higher"; the aggregator dropped one. The per-kg column is the binding figure for a heavy, low-energy pack. This is the third time in this project that going to the primary document changed the answer.

⚠ A second ruling exists that I do not have: Valuation Ruling 2061/2026 covers lithium-ion CELLS — a different item, and the item Route B actually imports. Its values were not obtained. That is now a required document, not an optional one.

The recommendation

"The companies using lead-acid batteries in electric motorcycles should be shut down." — Naz Baloch, committee member. Reasons given: substandard battery quality, and risk of battery explosions in hot weather.

⚠ No deadline, no company named, and no count of how many of the 34 licensees use lead-acid. This is a recommendation, not a rule — but it is the clearest signal yet of where policy is heading.

It validates the most load-bearing judgement in this dossier

This research concluded that lead-acid dies in 8–13 months at fleet duty in Pakistani heat, and found the tariff excludes lead-acid from the 1% concession by name while taxing it at double the lithium rate.

Parliament has independently arrived at the same place, for the same reason — heat. Policy, tariff and engineering now all point the same way. A lithium-only position is not a contrarian call in this market; it is the direction of travel.

🔵 The grey market — and it lands directly on Route B

Read the duty structure again and the threat inverts

The National Assembly hearing is explicit: the grey market exists because "high import duties on genuine lithium-ion batteries have made the economics attractive and the absence of enforcement has made it risk-free." Unlicensed operators refurbish cells imported as scrap and sell them unregistered and untested.

But those are the duties on finished packs — CD 10% + RD 2% — and on lead-acid at 20–24%. Route B imports CELLS at 0%. So a legitimate Route B operator faces the same zero duty the scrap importer evades, and beats them on cell quality, BMS, testing and warranty. The grey market's entire cost advantage is avoidance of a duty Route B does not pay.

⚠ Two things stop that being a clean win. The scrap operator also avoids PSQCA and any warranty obligation — and PSQCA has no lithium testing laboratory, so nothing forces the comparison at point of sale. And the customer cannot tell the difference when buying: a pack that dies at 2–3 years looks identical on the showroom floor. That is a marketing problem, not a cost problem — and it is exactly the problem a rental or BaaS model does not have, because the operator keeps the pack and bears the failure directly.

And one line that changes how EDB certification should be read

"EDB licences did not specifically require lithium-ion, allowing some companies to use cheaper lead-acid dry batteries."

🔴 So an EDB licence certifies the assembler, not the chemistry. The 34 licensees are not all lithium builders — and the PAVE approved-model list routes through EDB approval, so it may include lead-acid machines. That materially weakens any assumption that PAVE approval implies a quality floor. Reported outcomes bear it out: low-grade packs unusable within 2–3 years, replacement up to PKR 90,000.

From the committee record

"High import duties fuel illegal refurbishment operations. Unlicensed operators import lithium-ion cells as scrap and resell them without registration or quality control, undermining legitimate manufacturers."

Read that against the tariff section. Route B — lithium-ion cells at 0% duty — was identified as the most attractive entry, needing only Sales Tax registration and an IOCO quota. There is already an informal channel bringing the same cells in as scrap, at no duty and no compliance cost at all.

A legitimate importer on Route B pays 0% duty too — but also carries IOCO quota administration, PSQCA clearance, Sales Tax registration and quality liability, and then competes on price against product carrying none of it.

First evidence of an informal-sector competitor

It attacks the exact component that is 53% of FOB cost. It does not kill Route B — a fleet operator selling uptime is far less exposed than a battery retailer — but any plan to sell packs into the aftermarket now has a named competitor with a structural cost advantage.

🟢 One figure worth keeping: the committee heard that substandard batteries have 2–3 year lifespans and cost PKR 90,000 to replace — consistent with this dossier's own finding of PKR 115,000 Pakistani retail for a 72V 30Ah LiFePO4 pack, from an entirely independent source.

Registrations up 53–75% with support slashed to ₹5,000/vehicle. TVS 25%, Bajaj 23%, Ather 16%, Hero 11% — those four take 95.6% of incremental growth.

The three transferable lessons

1. Demand survives subsidy withdrawal. Entrants often do not. The market was captured by whoever already had dealers, service and brand trust. In Pakistan that means Atlas Honda — and it has begun.

2. Service is the decisive variable, not price. Ola had capital, product, brand and half the market. It lost on spare parts and service capacity — and a state regulator removed its right to sell.

3. Localisation-linked subsidy claims carry clawback risk. Pakistan's 1% duty concession is gated on EDB certification and annual input reconciliation. Same structural shape. In India, getting that paperwork wrong was company-ending.

The vehicle in law — there is no low-speed class

Provincial Motor Vehicles Ordinance 1965, read in full

"Motor cycle" has two tests only: fewer than four wheels, and unladen weight not exceeding 900 pounds (408 kg). There is no wattage, kW, cc or speed threshold anywhere, and section 23(1) is unconditional. Every electric two-wheeler in Pakistan is a motor cycle in law.

⚠ This corrects a claim repeated across Pakistani EV vendor sites — that machines below 250 W need no registration. It has no basis in the Ordinance and appears to be Indian rules restated. So the sub-PKR-97,000 tier cannot be unlocked by regulatory arbitrage — it would take amending primary legislation in four provinces.

19 — the certification gate

The gate behind the rate is written for a different technology

SRO 656(I)/2006 — the operative condition, confirmed from the primary text

The 1% is not automatic. It requires certification and quota determination by the Engineering Development Board, measured against SRO 656(I)/2006. That instrument contains no electric-vehicle provisions at all — its only use of the word "electric" describes a crane hoist.

The motorcycle schedule an e-bike assembler is judged against mandates an engine assembly line — crank cases, crankshaft and connecting rod, spark plug and tappet adjustment, oil filling — plus an emission tester at final inspection. An electric motorcycle has none of those. Certification therefore rests on how EDB applies an obsolete schedule; eligibility, cost and timeline cannot be self-assessed before applying.

Also required, and not optional

  • Sales Tax Act 1990 registration — a hard prerequisite
  • On-site facility verification
  • Component lists approved by EDB or IOCO and uploaded to WeBOC
  • Annual input reconciliation by 15 August, failing which the manufacturing certificate is not revalidated
  • PSQCA release certificate at import — samples may be lab-tested, and non-conforming shipments are destroyed
  • 🔴 PSQCA has established 172 standards for electric motorcycles — but the National Assembly panel records that it “needs to strengthen its technical capacity and establish dedicated lithium battery testing laboratories.” Standards exist on paper; the capability to verify lithium packs against them does not.

The incentive layer

  • NEV subsidy: PKR 65,000 per two-wheeler, tapering to 80/60/40% in years 3, 4 and 5
  • Commercial charging fixed at PKR 39.7/kWh under the NEV Policy
  • PAVE scheme — subsidised financing for e-bikes and e-rickshaws
  • Sales tax on locally manufactured EVs held at 1% (Eighth Schedule)
  • ⚠ Installed local capacity is ~2 million units/yr and "heavily underutilized" — the government's own words. That is the single strongest argument against adding assembly capacity
One on-ramp, status unconfirmed

AIDEP allowed 10 built-up units per variant at half duty, to a maximum 200 units per company, with manufacturing compulsory within two years. AIDEP expired 30 June 2026 and it is unconfirmed whether the two-wheeler pilot continued. It is the most useful provision found in the whole regime, and it may be extinct.

19 — what this does not establish

The honest boundary

  • Five supplier RFQs were sent on 27 August 2026 — three CKD kit makers and both battery suppliers — but no reply has been received, so every supplier figure here remains a published listing, not a quote. No bank, delivery platform or regulator has been contacted at all.
  • The scale of ELFA–Bykea and foodpanda–Wasl is undisclosed. Both answer whether a platform will finance rider EVs — neither discloses rider counts, unit counts or terms, and that number decides whether Karachi’s delivery segment is taken or barely touched.
  • How EDB applies an ICE-era facility schedule to an electric vehicle. SRO 656(I)/2006 contains no EV provisions at all — its only use of "electric" describes a crane hoist — yet the primary text confirms it as the operative condition. Its motorcycle schedule mandates an engine assembly line and an emission tester. An electric motorcycle has neither.
  • The kW→cc conversion factor. Balochistan names the mechanism without publishing the ratio, and it decides 1% against 2% of vehicle value.
  • Units per container, and dangerous-goods freight on lithium. Freight per unit is currently a container rate divided by an assumption.
  • The IOCO quota process for the 0% battery routes — timeline, cost, feasibility — is entirely unknown. IOCO is a different body from EDB.
  • The electricity connection behind a swap point is uncosted. Cabinet hardware is now priced at PKR 1.0–1.4m and imports duty-free, but 8504.4030 is a static converter line and a swap cabinet may classify elsewhere — and the K-Electric connection charge is quoted per site after survey, so it cannot be researched.
  • Pakistani market volumes are press-reported. PAMA counts members only, which excludes almost every electric maker. A penetration figure computed on that basis was withdrawn from this research.
  • No Pakistani brand publishes unit sales. The catalogues show what is offered, not what sells.
  • Three referenced instruments have not been read: SRO 693(I)/2006, SRO 655(I)/2006, and SRO 929(I)/2024 sr.3(xi) — whose own record in the government portal is self-contradictory on its expiry.
  • The battery policy is now the Next Generation Energy Storage Policy (2026–2033) and is still unpublished — wider in scope and later than earlier reporting implied. Real battery failure rates do not exist, so the warranty-exposure arithmetic rests on a guessed 15%.
  • No Pakistani residual-value series exists. The depreciation figures used are generic e-bike industry numbers and the used-price band is a listings range, not matched models — yet the fleet model’s exit depends on it.
  • How an insurer rates a zero-cc electric motorcycle is unknown, and no EV-specific motorcycle insurance product was found in Pakistan at all — a real exposure for a rental fleet carrying lithium-battery liability on assets it owns.
One methodological note worth keeping

Several conclusions in this research were reversed by primary sources after being drawn from trade press or official summaries — a market-share ranking that could not be reconciled with audited filings; a penetration figure computed across mismatched populations; a "10–15%" duty rate that the schedule states flat at 15%; a concession expiry that the printed schedule appeared to have missed. Where a filing or a statute disagreed with a summary, the primary source won every time. Any figure still carrying a Press grade should be treated as provisional.
