Who actually builds these, and who is already selling them in Pakistan

A market-entry brief for electric motorcycles and scooters — the segment that competes with the 70cc commuter. Built from public sources, with the strength of each figure marked.

Compiled 25 August 2026 · Desk research · No supplier contacted

Read this first

There is no list of 5,000 companies to find

The request was for 5,000 players. The industry does not contain them, and one number settles it: in 2025 the ten largest Chinese makers of licence-plated electric motorcycles shipped 94.0% of the entire national market. The remaining 6% is split among a long tail of small workshops and rebadgers with no published volumes, no export operation, and nothing to verify.

A 5,000-row list could be generated. It would be mostly trading-company listings scraped from B2B storefronts, self-declared and unverifiable — and it would read as authoritative while being noise. What follows is the part that is real and checkable.

Segment boundary — the most misread figure here

The 3.5m unit market above is licence-plated electric motorcycles — registered vehicles, the segment a Pakistani commuter product sits in. China's total electric two-wheeler market, including e-bikes and light mopeds, ran to 32.33 million units in the first half of 2025 alone, against an installed base near 380 million.

A company can be enormous in one and absent from the other. Yadea leads both; Aima leads the broad market but does not appear in the licence-plated top ten. Trade media quote the two interchangeably, and any ranking that mixes them is meaningless.

Naming traps — how a list like this silently inflates

Every one of these has produced duplicate rows in published “top 10” lists: Xinri = Sunra (新日, one company, two romanisations) · Tailing ≈ Tailg (台铃) · Aima / “Emma” (爱玛, same name rendered twice) · ZEEHO is CFMOTO's electric brand, not a separate maker · Segway and Ninebot have been one company since 2015.

Count the manufacturing entity, not the badge. This is the single mechanism by which a list reaches an impressive length without containing that many companies.

Also recorded, so they are not researched twice: Velotric, Urtopia, TENWAYS and Tezeus are Chinese-founded premium e-bicycle brands built for Europe and North America. Real businesses, wrong product, wrong market.

Company detail

What each of them is actually doing

The MOQ chasm — the number that reorders the shortlist

Huaihai, a top-five maker with its own OEM/ODM arm, quotes a minimum order of 10,000 units a year. At roughly US$350 FOB that is about US$3.5m — around twenty times the entire PKR 50m capital ceiling. The tier that will deal at 50–200 units is trading houses and small ODMs, some quoting as few as 1–5.

There is nothing in between. Scale and reachability are inversely related here, and the gap between them is exactly where a first-time entrant falls — because the reachable tier is also where the battery warranty backstop is weakest and hardest to enforce from Pakistan.

Yadea 雅迪 Pakistan taken

Converting domestic dominance into Southeast Asian manufacturing. 2025 revenue RMB 37.0bn (+31.1%), net profit RMB 2.91bn — up 128.8%, on 16.3 million units. Building a US$100m Vietnam plant at 1m units/year; Indonesia R&D since 2024; exports to 100+ countries. Moving up-market on sodium-ion batteries.

🔴 Its 921,100 licence-plated motorcycles are just 5.6% of its total volume — the league table everyone quotes measures a sliver of what these firms do.

Huaihai 淮海 Already here

Founded 1976. Three brands — Huaihai, Zongshen, Hoann — 27 subsidiaries, assets over RMB 10bn, a network across 96 countries, and overseas bases in Pakistan, India, Chile, Peru and Indonesia.

⚠️ Zongshen and Huaihai are entangled — Jiangsu Zongshen Vehicle was jointly established by both in 2003, and Jiangsu Zongshen Automobile is a Huaihai subsidiary. Ranks 3 and 4 may be one group: 810,000 units, 23.1%, near Yadea.

Luyuan 绿源 Worth asking

Competing on engineering, not price. Revenue ~RMB 5.91bn, net profit up 50%+, fifth largest by revenue. Differentiator is proprietary liquid-cooled motor technology sold on motor lifespan — a durability claim that suits Pakistani roads. Earlier in its export journey than Yadea, which may make it more approachable.

Aima 爱玛 Ask early

Regionalising right now. Vietnam plant in production, Indonesia commissioning — Yadea's path, one step behind. Leads the broad market but is absent from the licence-plated top ten, so its mix leans to e-bikes and mopeds. Either receptive to a South Asia conversation, or already forming its own plans. Worth reaching before those harden.

Why they are all suddenly interested in exporting

A new national standard took effect in November 2024, regulating batteries and chargers, and with a 2025 trade-in policy it is forcing a replacement peak at home. Lithium e-bikes rose from 4.62m units in 2024 to ~5.44m in 2025 as safety rules push lead-acid out. The domestic market is vast but maturing — 49.6m e-bikes sold in China in 2024 against a stock above 400 million.

🔴 Two consequences for Pakistan. The cheap lead-acid machines being regulated out of China are exactly what an emerging market absorbs — expect them to be offered, and expect the warranty exposure that travels with them. And battery swapping is where the Chinese industry is actually investing (US$460m in 2025, heading to US$1.25bn by 2031, 73.85% lithium) — while Pakistan's own NEV Policy plans swapping stations for two- and three-wheelers. That is a business adjacent to vehicles that does not require competing with two million units of idle assembly capacity.

Demand side

Pakistan is not an emerging market for this. It is an exploding one.

A single quarter of 2026 came within a fifth of the whole of 2025. The overall two-wheeler market hit an all-time high of 1.93 million units in FY26, with the first half of 2026 up 33.8% — so electric is growing against a rising base, not cannibalising a flat one.

The government's own New Energy Vehicles Policy 2025–30 targets 116,053 electric two-wheelers in 2025-26 rising to 740,898 by 2029-30, and sets cost-sharing at a reference PKR 65,000 per two-wheeler against a five-year budget of PKR 85 billion.

Read the targets with the policy's own admission attached

The policy opens by stating that “the 2019 policy failed to meet its five years targets” and that its supply-side measures “have not been fully implemented till now.” A target set by a ministry that missed the last one is an intention, not a forecast. It must never become a demand assumption in a cost model. The subsidy is also designed to shrink — to 80% of face value in year three, 60% in year four, 40% in year five.

The current tariff structure — found, and it is a one-year bridge

The NEV Policy says AIDEP 2021–26 incentives ran “till the end of the policy period i.e June 30, 2026”, which reads like a cliff already passed. It is not: the Finance Act 2026 extended the EV concessions by one year. FBR's own budget document says “Extension in exemption on import of CKD for electric vehicles till 30.06.2027” and, in its own words, “Extension in date of sunset for electric vehicles till 30.06.2027.”

For electric motorcycles — PCT 8711.6040 — the rate is 1% customs duty on EV-specific components in kit form, with 10–15% on non-localised CKD parts, under Table-II to Part-V(A) of the Fifth Schedule to the Customs Act 1969.

The gate behind that rate is written for a different century

The 1% is not automatic — it requires “certification and quota determination by the Engineering Development Board”, measured against the facility schedule in SRO 656(I)/2006. That schedule contains no electric-vehicle provisions at all. The only occurrence of the word “electric” in the entire instrument describes a crane hoist.

The motorcycle schedule an e-bike assembler would be judged against mandates an “ENGINE ASSEMBLY & TESTING” line with fixtures for “Crank Cases”, “Complete Cylinder with Crankshaft and Connecting Rod”, “Spindle Kick Starter”, “Spark Plug, Rocker Arm and Tappet adjusting” and “Oil Filling” — plus an emission tester at final inspection.

An electric motorcycle has none of those things. So certification rests on how EDB chooses to apply an obsolete instrument, not on a published standard you can measure yourself against. You cannot self-assess eligibility, cost or timeline before applying — and one applicant's acceptance does not bind the next. This is the question to ask before anything else in the project proceeds. It may also explain why 50+ licences have been issued while only about six holders reportedly manufacture.

And the bridge ends on 30 June 2027, roughly ten months out. The successor policy — AIDEP 2026-31 — is still a draft awaiting both the Prime Minister and the IMF, with manufacturers contesting its tariff cuts, and published analysis of the draft notes two-wheelers are not separately addressed in it. Meanwhile FBR is pruning the Fifth Schedule, deleting entries “where concessionary CD equals or exceeds the First Schedule general tariff.” Model the case twice — with the concessions and with none.

The incumbents

Who is already importing and assembling in Pakistan

This is the closest available answer to “who is bringing the units in”. Shipment-level customs records — which exporter shipped to which importer — sit behind paid subscriptions and were deliberately not bought. What follows is the licensed and publicly-trading field instead.

The licensing picture

The Engineering Development Board has issued 50+ manufacturing licences, mostly for electric bikes and rickshaws. At an earlier count of 31 licences, only about six were actually manufacturing.

Press reporting puts roughly 84 companies in the space — a mix of shoestring operations, venture-backed startups and existing industrial groups. A licence is not a factory.

The confirmed China → Pakistan link

Yadea — the world's largest maker — is already in Pakistan, via a partnership with Road Prince, with the T5 retailing around PKR 245,000.

Checked against PAMA and EDB — and one figure did not survive

PAMA does not count these companies. Its membership was checked directly: of every electric two-wheeler name above, only Sazgar is a member. Jolta, Vlektra, MS Jaguar, Road Prince, Metro, Pakzon, Crown, Okla and EcoDost are all absent, so their volumes are not in PAMA's statistics.

An earlier version of this brief put electric penetration at “~8–9% of the two-wheeler market”. That figure is withdrawn. It divided a press-sourced electric number by a total-market number that is essentially PAMA-member sales — overwhelmingly combustion, and excluding almost every electric maker. Numerator and denominator came from different populations, which makes the ratio meaningless however reasonable it looked.

The EDB publishes no licence register that could be found. The counts in circulation — “31 licences, about six actually manufacturing”, later “50+” — are press attributions to EDB officials, not documents. They may be right; they are not sourced.

D

Jiangsu Huaihai MOQ blocks the obvious route

An earlier version of this brief ranked Huaihai as the most plausible target. Its OEM/ODM minimum is 10,000 units a year — roughly twenty times the capital ceiling — so that route is closed. But it already operates a base in Pakistan, which makes the useful question not “will you supply me” but “what are you already doing here, and does it need a local partner?”

E

The ODM and trading tier The only tier that fits

Wuxi MDKA, Wuxi Legend, Zhejiang Taiya and the wider Alibaba layer quote CKD and SKD kits at 50–200 units, some as low as 1–5, with production in 15–25 days and sea freight in 25–35. It is the only tier that fits the capital available — and the tier where a battery warranty backstop is weakest, which is precisely what K5 exists to catch.

Correction — the obvious plan does not survive the primary source

An earlier version of this brief concluded that CKD assembly with a mid-tier or ODM partner was the configuration that survived every constraint. Reading the NEV Policy directly removes that conclusion. The policy states that “local assembly and production capacity of electric two and three-wheeler has risen to two 2 million vehicles per annum. However, much of this capacity is heavily underutilized.”

Pakistan does not need another assembly line. It has roughly two million units a year of installed capacity standing idle, and the government's demand-side measures exist to fill capacity that already exists. Adding supply to a market whose stated problem is underutilisation is not a strategy — and the fact that idle capacity has not already captured this opportunity is the single most important thing to understand before committing anything.

So where does that leave the entry question

If capacity is not scarce, the scarce things are what capacity cannot supply: demand generation, distribution reach, brand trust, after-sales and parts, and consumer financing. An entry thesis should be built on one of those, or on using someone else's idle line rather than building one — a far lighter call on capital, and one that fits the constraints better than importing or assembling ever did.

That reframing does not weaken the case. It relocates it. But it cannot be confirmed until a manufacturer is actually asked.

One documented on-ramp worth confirming

AIDEP 2021-26 allowed 10 built-up units per variant at half the prevailing customs duty, up to 200 units per company, across scooters, motorcycles, rickshaws and loaders — with “subsequent manufacturing within 2 years of import” made compulsory, EDB recommending and MoIP approving.

Import a couple of hundred units at half duty, prove the market, then commit to building. That fits a capital-constrained first entry far better than either full CBU importing or a plant from a standing start. 🔴 But AIDEP expired on 30 June 2026 and it is unconfirmed whether the two-wheeler pilot survived — only the four-wheeler version is known to continue. It is the most useful provision found so far, and it may no longer exist.

Limits

What this brief does not establish

  • No supplier has been contacted. Every availability judgement above is inference from public evidence. Whether a manufacturer will supply you is answerable only by asking.
  • No shipment-level records. Which exporter shipped to which Pakistani importer requires a paid customs-data subscription that was deliberately not purchased.
  • The Chinese table is one segment. Licence-plated electric motorcycles, calendar 2025. Not e-bikes, not mopeds, not the broader tens-of-millions market.
  • Pakistani market figures remain press-reported. PAMA's own data files were not obtained — only its membership, which is what exposed the denominator problem. A defensible penetration rate needs registration data, where numerator and denominator count the same population.
  • The Fifth Schedule text itself has not been read — only FBR's budget summary and the Board of Investment's description of it. The exact component list and serial numbers still need checking at source.
  • How EDB applies an ICE-shaped facility schedule to an electric two-wheeler is unknown — and it gates the 1% rate. No published standard exists to self-assess against.
  • Whether the 200-unit two-wheeler CBU pilot survived the AIDEP expiry is unconfirmed.
  • PKR 65,000 versus PKR 80,000 is unresolved. The policy says 65,000; press reporting says 80,000 for a separate programme. Until that is settled, only the policy figure is sourced.

Official bilateral trade statistics were also pulled for this work. They are held privately in the project workspace and are deliberately not reproduced here — the licence governing them prohibits redistribution.
