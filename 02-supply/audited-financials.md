## 🔴 Finding 1 — the industry league table cannot be reconciled with audited accounts

The ranking every trade publication reprints — and which this project reproduced — puts the
2025 licence-plated electric motorcycle market at **3,506,200 units**, with a top ten holding
94%. Set that against what the companies themselves filed:

| | Audited own filing | Credited in the ranking |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Aima** — electric two-wheeled motorcycles | **2,615,982** | **absent from the top ten entirely** |
| **Xinri** — electric two-wheeled motorcycles | **459,833** | 262,900 |

**Aima's audited motorcycle volume alone is 74.6% of the entire stated market**, and Aima does
not appear in the table. **Xinri's own accounts are 75% higher** than the figure attributed to
it. Two companies alone report **3,075,815** units against a "total market" of 3,506,200.

🔴 **Do not use that ranking.** The discrepancy is probably definitional — "licence-plated"
almost certainly counts something narrower than what companies call electric two-wheeled
motorcycles, and association tables cover members — but **the reconciliation is unknown, so the
table cannot support a decision.** Audited filings are the only volume source that survives
scrutiny.

## 🔴 Finding 2 — these are domestic Chinese businesses with an export gesture attached

This is the single most decision-relevant thing in the filings, and it is invisible in press
coverage.

| Company | International business, per its own audited accounts |
|---|---|
| **Aima** | Overseas revenue **RMB 216.2m — 0.86% of revenue — and it FELL 7.89%** in 2025 |
| **Xinri** | Export revenue **RMB 101.9m — 2.48% of revenue**, at **9.95% gross margin** |
| **NIU** | **80,018 units international of 1,192,039 — 6.7%**, across partners in 40+ countries |
| **Luyuan** | IPO proceeds for international expansion — **HKD 6.1m, "delayed and unutilized"** |
| **Yadea** | The exception: a live US$100m plant in Bắc Ninh, Vietnam |

**Aima is the clearest case.** Press describes it as aggressively regionalising, with plants in
Vietnam and Indonesia — both true, both in the filing. But **overseas is under one percent of
revenue and shrinking.** The plants are new and the export business is currently negligible.

**Xinri exports at roughly half its domestic margin** — 9.95% against 14–21% across its Chinese
regions. Exporting is its *least* profitable channel, even growing at +132%.

**NIU averages about 2,000 units per foreign market per year** despite four decades of partners
in 40+ countries.

### What this means for the entry question

**If you want a Chinese partner with real export capability and appetite, the field is
essentially Yadea — whose Pakistan territory is already taken — and the ODM/trading tier.**
Everyone else is a domestic Chinese business. A partner for whom export is under 1% of revenue
and falling will not fund your warranty claims, your marketing, or your credit terms. That bears
directly on **K5** and **K11**.

## Finding 3 — Yadea sells more batteries than vehicles

From the audited FY2025 revenue table:

| Product | Revenue RMB'000 | % | Volume '000 units |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Electric bicycles | 16,208,695 | 43.8% | 11,453.5 |
| Electric scooters | 9,045,907 | 24.4% | 4,815.7 |
| *Vehicles subtotal* | *25,254,602* | *68.2%* | *16,269.2* |
| **Batteries and chargers** | **10,523,089** | **28.4%** | **Batteries 18,275.1 · Chargers 17,005.8** |
| E-2W parts | 1,230,545 | 3.4% | — |
| **Total** | **37,008,236** | 100.0% | 51,550.1 |

**18.3 million batteries against 16.3 million vehicles.** Batteries and chargers are **RMB
10.5bn — 28.4% of revenue.** The recurring money in this industry is batteries, not machines,
and it reframes **K5**: the market leader *monetises* battery replacement rather than absorbing
it as warranty cost. Luyuan's accounts show the same shape — a separate line for parts "sold
separately to distributors for their provision of after-sales services."

## Finding 4 — profitability splits the field in two

| | Revenue | Gross margin | Net result | Net margin | Vehicle units |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Yadea** FY25 | RMB 37.01bn (+31.1%) | 19.11% | **+RMB 2.91bn** | **7.87%** | 16,269,200 |
| **Aima** FY25 | RMB 25.10bn (+16.14%) | — | **+RMB 2.04bn** | **8.11%** | 11,639,817 |
| **Luyuan** FY24 | RMB 5.07bn (flat) | 13.1% | +RMB 116.1m | 2.29% | — |
| **Xinri** FY25 | RMB 4.11bn (+17.15%) | — | +RMB 50.6m | **1.23%** | 2,284,597 |
| **NIU** FY25 | — | 19.6% | 🔴 **−RMB 39.4m** | loss | 1,192,039 |

- **Yadea and Aima are the only substantial profit pools** — ~8% net each. Yadea's profit rose
  **128.8%**; Aima's rose 2.34%.
- **NIU has lost money three years running** — RMB 271.8m, 193.2m, 39.4m — at essentially the
  same gross margin as Yadea. **Scale, not pricing, separates them.**
- **Luyuan's five audited years:** RMB 2.38bn (2020) → 3.42bn → 4.78bn → 5.08bn → **5.07bn
  (2024)** — growth stalled, profit fell from RMB 145.6m to 116.1m. FY2025 recovered to RMB
  5.91bn and RMB 175.1m, still under 3% net.
- **Xinri at 1.23% net margin has essentially no cushion.**

## Manufacturing footprints, per the filings

- **Yadea** — Wuxi plus seven domestic plants; **US$100m Bắc Ninh, Vietnam plant inaugurated
  early 2026**, 1m units/year, for "local demand and exports". Named markets: Vietnam,
  Indonesia, Philippines. 🔴 **Pakistan is not named.**
- **Aima** — Tianjin, Chongqing and other domestic bases; **Aima Vietnam and two wholly-owned
  Indonesian subsidiaries (PT AIMA Electric Vehicles Indonesia, PT AIMA Tech Indonesia) have
  both commenced operations.** Cites Vietnam's motorcycle restrictions and Indonesian
  fuel-to-electric subsidies as the draw.
- **Xinri** — four domestic bases (Wuxi, Tianjin, Xiangyang, Guangdong) plus an Indonesian
  operation being opened; overseas assets RMB 126.8m, **3.70% of total assets.**

**Every one of them is pointed at Southeast Asia. None names Pakistan.**
