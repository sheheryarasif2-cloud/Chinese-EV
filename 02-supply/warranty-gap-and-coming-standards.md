### The gap, stated plainly

**Pakistani competitors warrant batteries for 2 to 5 years. The reachable Chinese supplier warrants
1 year on the relevant pack size.**

🔴 **That is K5, quantified.** An importer buying at Koyosonic's price cannot match ELFA's
3-year/50,000 km — the company that holds Bykea — **without self-insuring the difference.**
The gap is **two years of battery liability**, on a component costing **PKR 59,281–115,000**, across
however many machines are in the fleet.

**Do the arithmetic on 200 machines.** If even 15% of packs fail between year 1 and year 3 and must
be honoured at ~PKR 60,000, that is **PKR 1.8m of unfunded warranty exposure** — and 15% is a guess,
because no failure-rate data exists. `[PLACEHOLDER: real failure rates]`

🟢 **Two things soften it, and both are real:**
1. **A rental or BaaS operator does not warrant anything to a rider** — it simply keeps the fleet
   running. The exposure becomes an **operating cost**, which is forecastable, rather than a
   **contingent liability**, which is not. **This is now the strongest structural argument for
   rental over sale.**
2. **Hunan CTS publishes 1,500 cycles @ 90% DOD** and holds ISO 9001 + IATF 16949 — so a longer
   warranty may be negotiable from a supplier that stands behind its cycle life. **That is exactly
   what the RFQs asked.**

⚠️ **But note what "warranty" means at this tier.** `audited-financials.md` established that for
Yadea, **batteries and chargers are 28.4% of revenue** and it sells **more batteries than
vehicles** — the industry monetises replacement rather than absorbing it. **A generous warranty from
a Chinese ODM is a marketing number until someone tests who actually honours a claim from Karachi.**

## 🔴 The battery policy is NOT out — and it has been rebranded and widened

Earlier reporting said a battery policy was *"being finalised."* As of August 2026 it is:

> **The Next Generation Energy Storage Policy (2026–2033)** — EDB Chief Executive **Hammad Mansoor**
> expected to present an upgraded version at a stakeholders' meeting *"shortly."*

**So it is still in development, its scope has widened from EV batteries to energy storage
generally, and its horizon now runs to 2033.** 🔴 **It remains the single most consequential
pending document for this decision, and it is later than the July reporting implied.**

### The proposed standards, and they are demanding

The Ministry of Industries has **directed PSQCA to begin developing standards for lithium-ion
vehicle batteries**, noting that EV packs need far more than the existing consumer-battery
standards because they run at higher voltages and capacities. Proposed requirements reported:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Road durability / drop test** | Packs must withstand a **one-metre drop onto bare concrete** with **no structural cracks, leaks or internal damage** |
| 🔴 **Ingress protection** | **Dust protection, weatherproofing and water resistance — explicitly including flooding and monsoon conditions** |
| Verification | **EDB + NEECA** directed to build an inspection, testing and verification mechanism |

🟢 **This is directly actionable and should go into any RFQ now, not later.** The RFQs already
asked suppliers for **IP rating** — that was the right question. **They should also be asked
specifically whether the pack passes a 1 m drop test onto concrete**, because a pack that cannot
will be unsellable once the standard lands, and **a fleet in Karachi faces monsoon flooding every
year regardless of what any standard says.**

⚠️ **Karachi floods.** A pack rated for dust but not immersion is the wrong pack for this city, and
that is an engineering judgement this research can make now without waiting for a policy.
